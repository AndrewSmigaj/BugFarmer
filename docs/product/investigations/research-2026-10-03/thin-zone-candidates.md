# Candidate bugs for thin zones (2026-10-03)

These are tentative suggestions for the owner, not decisions or game data.

How this was checked: each candidate was read on its Wikipedia page (and other pages where named), fetched on
2026-10-03; anything the pages did not state is listed under "Not confirmed". The game's rules used for the fit
checks come from `docs/gdd/00_premise.md`: fish and people survive; mammals, birds, reptiles and amphibians are gone;
there are no worms or leeches, and snails go by the same rule (`03_bestiary.md`). So a fish-eating habit is fine, but
a life that needs frogs, tadpoles, lizards, birds, mammals, snails or earthworms is flagged. The game has no seasons,
so anything tied to a season is flagged too. Species the owner already cut, or already on the decided roster
(`docs/gdd/bug_lineups.jsonl`), are not proposed again as new species; one option in section 7 is a new role for a
roster species, and says so.

## 1. Bee Meadow (beekeeping, the coast and its beach)

### 1a. Hummingbird hawk-moth — Macroglossum stellatarum — fits
- **Eats:** the adult drinks nectar from tube-shaped flowers while hovering, with a 25–28 mm tongue (red valerian,
  honeysuckle, jasmine, buddleia, lilac, verbena, viper's bugloss, phlox and others). Caterpillars eat bedstraws and
  madders, and have been recorded on chickweed (Stellaria), willowherb and red valerian.
- **Breeds:** a female lays up to 200 tiny green eggs, one per plant; they hatch in 6–8 days. Two or more broods a
  year, three or four in the south. Adults sit out the winter in cracks in rocks, trees and buildings.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) flies by day, mostly in bright sun but also at dawn, dusk and in rain, hovering
  with an audible hum, its wings a blur that can look orange; (2) it "trap-lines": it returns to the same flower
  beds at about the same time each day, so a player can learn its round; (3) it learns flower colours well.
- **Danger to people:** none recorded on the pages read.
- **Habitat and fit:** gardens, parks, meadows, scrub, woodland edges, heath and coast; it breeds mainly in warm
  climates and reaches northern Europe as a summer migrant. Fits the Bee Meadow well.
- **Needs gone animals?** No. Its caterpillars need bedstraw or madder plants, which the game would have to grow.
- **Adds:** a gentle daytime moth with a daily round you can learn and wait for, the day-flying opposite of the
  death's-head hawkmoth that raids hives there at night (both are hawk-moths).
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hummingbird_hawk-moth ;
  https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/invertebrates/moths/hummingbird-hawk-moth
- **Not confirmed:** its predators; any use people make of it; whether it competes with bees for the same flowers.

### 1b. European beewolf — Philanthus triangulum — fits, and is the strongest Bee Meadow candidate
- **Eats:** adults drink nectar (bramble, sea-holly, heather, thrift, ragwort, thistle). A female also presses a
  caught bee's honey stomach until a drop of nectar comes out of its mouth, and licks it up (German Wikipedia). The
  larvae eat paralysed honey bees: 1–5 per cell (English page), 3–4 and up to 6 (German page); one female may
  catch up to 100 bees during her flight period.
- **Hunts:** takes worker honey bees at flowers, stings them through the soft joint behind the front legs, and flies
  home with the paralysed bee held between her legs. Honey bees are its main prey everywhere; in Britain it has also
  taken a few solitary bees. BWARS: "in some regions the wasp may greatly reduce the local populations".
- **Breeds:** digs burrows up to 1 m long in sandy ground or in banks of soil in open sunny places, with 3–34 side
  tunnels each ending in a cell; burrows gather in colonies of up to 15,000. She smears each cell with a white paste
  from her antennae full of Streptomyces bacteria; they coat the cocoon and protect the young from mould. One brood a
  year in the north (July to September), two in central Europe; the young spend the winter in the cell.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) finds its own burrow by sight, from landmarks (Tinbergen's famous experiments);
  (2) picks bees off flowers one at a time rather than raiding the hive; (3) males hold small territories, about a
  quarter of a square metre, marked with scent from glands in the head.
- **Danger to people:** not stated on any page read.
- **Habitat and fit:** open sandy ground, lowland heath and coastal dunes; warm, dry heaths and thin grassland;
  lately even a town park and a London roundabout. Scandinavia to South Africa. The Bee Meadow's coves have a sand
  beach and a dune belt (`docs/product/zones/bee_meadow_20.md`), which fits.
- **Needs gone animals?** No. It needs honey bees, which the game has. Its enemy is a cuckoo wasp, Hedychrum rutilans.
- **Adds:** a real, visible enemy of the player's hives that works the flowers rather than the hive, with a nest
  field in the dunes a beekeeper can find; the honey-licking and the germ-killing bacteria are good description facts.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_beewolf ; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bienenwolf_(Wespe) ;
  https://www.bwars.com/wasp/crabronidae/philanthinae/philanthus-triangulum
- **Size:** females 13–18 mm, males 8–10 mm (German page).
- **Not confirmed:** how painful its sting is to people; whether it can wipe out a managed hive (only "greatly reduce
  the local populations" is stated); what beekeepers do about it.

### 1c. Dark-edged bee-fly — Bombylius major — does not fit as it stands
- **Eats:** adults drink nectar through a long, stiff beak while hovering, holding the flower with the front legs,
  and eat pollen. The larvae live in the nests of solitary bees and wasps, "particularly digging bees such as
  Andrena", eating the stored food and then the young bee.
- **Breeds:** the female hovers over a nest hole, coats her eggs with dust and flicks them in; the hatched larvae
  crawl into the nest or ride in on a bee. Adults fly in spring only (late March to June).
- **Behaviour:** a furry bee look-alike that hovers and turns on the spot; its long beak looks fierce but is harmless.
- **Danger to people:** "does not bite, sting, or spread disease."
- **Habitat:** temperate Europe, North America and parts of Asia, in places "from arid to moist"; 6.3–12 mm long.
  Needs no gone animals.
- **Why it fails:** its young need ground-nesting solitary bees, and the owner cut mason, leafcutter and sweat bees;
  no solitary bee is on the roster, so it would have nothing to breed on. It is also a spring-only fly in a game with
  no seasons.
- **Better for the same slot — the bumblebee hoverfly, Volucella bombylans:** a harmless fly that copies the
  buff-tailed bumblebee (the game's own bumblebee) and the red-tailed bumblebee. The female lays her eggs in the nests
  of bumblebees and social wasps, where the larvae live as scavengers on the nest's rubbish and "occasionally" eat
  the young bees. Adults take nectar and pollen, preferring blue flowers. Hedgerows, woodland edges, wet meadows and
  gardens; 11–17 mm long. **Adds:** a look-alike that teaches players to tell a fly from a bee, and a lodger in the
  player's bumblebee boxes. **Not confirmed:** how she gets into a nest unharmed; that it cannot bite.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombylius_major ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volucella_bombylans ;
  https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/invertebrates/flies/bumblebee-plumehorn
- **Not confirmed:** whether Bombylius major ever uses bumblebee or honey bee nests (no page named either).

## 2. Ant Tunnels and Ant Colony (home of the black garden ant, Lasius niger)

### 2a. Ant cricket — Myrmecophilus acervorum — fits
- **Eats:** ant crickets as a family "obtain food by soliciting trophallaxis in their host ants or by scraping off
  waxes from the bodies of ants": they beg food mouth-to-mouth from the ants or scrape the waxy coat off them. For
  this species the German page says only that it "presumably eats everything usable in the nest", from a few
  laboratory observations. What the young eat is not stated.
- **Breeds:** inside the ant nest; females lay eggs one at a time, at least 24 hours apart, without mating
  (parthenogenesis). Growing to adult takes two years.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) it touches workers again and again to take on the colony's smell; after about a
  week the ants accept it as one of their own; (2) put into a strange nest it keeps quiet and runs off fast when an ant
  touches it; (3) it is wingless, its eyes are under-developed, and it finds its way in the dark with its feelers.
- **Danger to people:** not stated.
- **Habitat and fit:** only in ant nests, with more than 20 ant species of the genera Lasius, Formica, Myrmica,
  Tetramorium, Tapinoma and Camponotus; in parks, woods, dry grassland, gravel pits, waste ground, field edges and
  towns; central and southern Europe. Fits the Ant Tunnels and Ant Colony.
- **Needs gone animals?** No; it needs ants, which the game has.
- **Adds:** a thief that lives inside the player's ant colony, eating its food and passing as a nest-mate, so an ant
  rancher has a real pest to find and pick out of the nest.
- **Size:** 2.5–4 mm (German page); females 3 mm (Polish page). Wingless.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmecophilus_acervorum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmecophilidae ;
  https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ameisengrille ; https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmecophilus_acervorum
- **Not confirmed:** that it lives with Lasius niger in particular (the pages name the genus Lasius, not the species);
  whether males exist (the German page says none has been found, the Polish page gives males up to 1.5 mm); how much
  food it really takes from a colony; whether it eats ant eggs or young.

### 2b. Ant-nest club beetle — Claviger testaceus — fits loosely: the black garden ant is not its main host
- **Which ant:** its main host is the yellow meadow ant, Lasius flavus; it is found "sometimes" with Lasius niger and
  Lasius alienus, and has also been found with Myrmica, Tetramorium and other Lasius ants (Polish Wikipedia). The
  German page also places it with the yellow meadow ant, and a cited study is about yellow meadow ant workers feeding
  it mouth-to-mouth.
- **Eats:** fed by its host ants (the study above). What else it eats is not stated on the pages read.
- **Breeds:** not stated.
- **Behaviour worth playing (shown for its genus):** every Claviger beetle depends entirely on its ants; "the ants
  feed it and carry it to safety with their mandibles when in danger"; it has tufts of hair that give off a liquid the
  ants take (German page on its sister species, C. longicornis, which it says "appears to act as an intoxicant rather
  than food"). Blind, with shrunken mouthparts, in the sister species.
- **Danger to people:** not stated.
- **Habitat and fit:** sunny, warm slopes across nearly all of Europe and Asia Minor; obligatory ant guest. Size
  2–2.5 mm.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds:** a blind beetle the ants feed and rescue, so a player who breaks into a nest sees workers carry it away
  first: a strange, real sight. But it belongs with an ant the game doesn't have.
- **Sources:** https://pl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claviger_testaceus ; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claviger_longicornis
  (reached from "Keulenkäfer") ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claviger_(beetle) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pselaphinae
- **Not confirmed:** whether C. testaceus itself is blind and wingless and gives the "intoxicant" liquid (those facts
  were stated for its genus or its sister species); what it eats beyond the ants' feeding; how common it is in black
  garden ant nests.

### 2c. A better guest for the black garden ant (not a beetle): the silver-studded blue — Plebejus argus — fits
- **Which ant:** found while checking the black garden ant's page, which names this butterfly as a guest. Black
  garden ants (Lasius niger) and Lasius alienus are its partners.
- **Eats:** caterpillars eat plants such as crown vetch (Coronilla), clover (Trifolium), bladder-senna (Colutea),
  broom (Genista), rock-rose (Helianthemum) and heathers. The ants "feed on a saccharine secretion produced by glands
  on the larvae".
- **Breeds:** mothers lay their eggs where the ants' scent is strong; the ants guard the eggs from predators and
  parasites, then "chaperone the larvae", driving off wasps and spiders. The caterpillars "usually spend the day in
  the nests of the ants", and the ants carry them into the nest to pupate. Two broods a year in the south, one in
  the north.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) ants and caterpillars trade sugar for protection; (2) the caterpillars shelter in
  the ant nest by day; (3) adults are "very sedentary, only moving around 20 metres" a day.
- **Danger to people:** not stated (a butterfly).
- **Habitat and fit:** heathland, mossland and limestone grassland across Europe and Asia. Fits the Ant Tunnels, which
  are half outdoors.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds:** with aphids cut, a real sugar-giving partner the black ants can herd: plant clover or heather by a
  colony and the ants raise the blues, a sandbox loop for an ant rancher.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasius_niger ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver-studded_blue
- **Caution:** aphids were cut over doubt that they add to play without muddling it (D79, `bug_table.jsonl`); this
  is the same kind of ant partnership, so it may meet the same doubt.
- **Not confirmed:** the adult's size.

## 3. Hilltop Meadow (advanced beekeeping; bees, bumblebees, hornets)

### 3a. A bee-hunting robber fly — the two named candidates fail; Mallophora ruficauda fits
- **Hornet robber fly, Asilus crabroniformis — fails:** "It is reliant on the availability of rabbit or cattle dung";
  its larvae are believed to eat dung beetle larvae. With mammals gone there is no dung. It also isn't a bee
  specialist: adults take grasshoppers, dung beetles and other flies. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asilus_crabroniformis)
- **Promachus "bee killers" — can't be checked:** the genus page and the red-footed cannibalfly (Promachus rufipes)
  page are stubs with no biology. (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promachus_(fly) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promachus_rufipes)
- **Better: Mallophora ruficauda, a bee-killer robber fly of Argentina's grasslands.**
  - **Eats:** adults hunt insects, mostly bees, wasps and their kin, and eat worker honey bees; in years when they
    are many "they can cause up to 80% losses of honey production" in the honey country of the Pampas. Larvae feed
    only on white grubs, the soil-dwelling young of scarab beetles, preferably the chafer Cyclocephala signaticollis.
  - **Breeds:** females lay clusters of about 300 eggs on the tips of tall grass stalks or wire fences, 1.25–1.5 m
    up; the hatchlings drift down on the wind, dig in, and home in on a grub by its smell. Several larvae often share
    one grub, but they prefer grubs not yet taken. Adults come out about two months after the eggs hatch.
  - **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) like all robber flies it waits on a perch and catches prey in flight, injecting
    saliva that paralyses and digests it, a stiff bristly "beard" guarding its face; (2) it looks like a bumblebee
    (black fur, a yellow patch, a reddish tail tip); (3) males patrol fixed routes and females rest on twigs; it is
    busiest on hot, sunny days.
  - **Danger to people:** robber flies "may deliver intensely painful bites to humans if handled incautiously" (the
    family page); nothing specific for this species.
  - **Habitat and fit:** open grassland and meadows; South and Central America. Fits a sunny hilltop meadow.
  - **Needs gone animals?** No. But its young need white grubs in the meadow soil, and no chafer is on the roster.
  - **Adds:** a real enemy of the advanced beekeeper that picks bees off in the air, looks like the bumblebees it
    lives among, and lays its eggs on fence tops and tall grass, where a beekeeper can find the clusters before
    the next generation hatches.
  - **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallophora_ruficauda ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asilidae
  - **Not confirmed:** its size; how many bees one fly eats; whether it takes bumblebees; what beekeepers do about it.

### 3b. Red velvet ant ("cow killer") — Dasymutilla occidentalis — fails; the European velvet ant fits instead
- **What it parasitises:** "the brood cells of Eastern cicada killers and horse guard wasps as well as other large
  ground-nesting members of Crabronidae": it sneaks into the nest and lays an egg on the host's young, which its grub
  eats. Not bees, on this page.
- **Why it fails:** the cicada killer stocks its nest with cicadas, which the owner cut; the horse guard wasp stocks
  its nest with horse flies (the game has the horse fly, but in the swamps). Neither host wasp is in the game, and
  neither belongs to the Hilltop Meadow's bees. It is also American (Connecticut to Kansas, Florida to Texas).
- **Its real facts, for the record:** sting ranked 3 of 4 on the Schmidt pain index, hence "cow killer"; females are
  wingless, males winged and stingless; it defends itself with a thick shell, fast dodging runs, warning colours, a
  squeaking sound, a chemical and venom; about 1.9 cm long.
- **Better for the same slot — the large velvet ant, Mutilla europaea:**
  - **Eats:** "Various bumblebees in the genus Bombus are the main hosts", and it has "infrequently" been found in
    honey bee hives. The female gets into the bumblebee nest and lays an egg in a cocoon holding a young bee; her grub
    eats it. A newly emerged adult feeds on the host bees' honey stores; the winged males sometimes drink nectar.
  - **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) a wingless female runs across sandy paths and walks into bumblebee nests; (2)
    when threatened it squeaks by rubbing a ridge on its body; (3) it is "very strong and armoured with a thick skin"
    (velvet ants in general take 11 times the force a honey bee does to crush).
  - **Danger to people:** "a painful sting", though its venom is "much less toxic than the venom of a honeybee"; "not
    aggressive and only sting people if handled". Only females sting.
  - **Habitat and fit:** heaths, moors, chalk grassland and woodland; most of Europe to China, Central Asia, the
    Middle East and North Africa. The game's buff-tailed bumblebee is in the Hilltop Meadow, so its host is there.
  - **Needs gone animals?** No.
  - **Adds:** a quiet threat to the player's bumblebee boxes that walks in on foot rather than flying, armoured and
    squeaking, so a beekeeper learns to watch the ground as well as the sky.
  - **Season note:** males die soon after they emerge in autumn and females sit out the winter; the game has no seasons.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasymutilla_occidentalis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphecius_speciosus ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stictia_carolina ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutilla_europaea ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutillidae
- **Not confirmed:** the size of Mutilla europaea (the page says only that it depends on the host's size); how much
  harm it does to a bumblebee colony; whether bumblebees fight it off.

## 4. Centipede Cavern and Underground River

### 4a. Whip spider (tailless whip scorpion) — Phrynus longipes, a cave-going whip spider — fits a warm cave
- **The group (Amblypygi):** no venom and no silk; they "rarely bite if threatened but can grab fingers with their
  pedipalps, resulting in thorn-like puncture injuries". Sit-and-wait hunters: they feel for prey with a very long,
  whip-like first pair of legs and spear it on the spiny grabbing arms (pedipalps). Some species live underground and
  have reduced or no eyes; all hunt at night; tropical and subtropical, in warm, damp places (leaf litter, caves,
  under bark).
- **Eats (P. longipes):** mostly small insects and other arthropods, sometimes small lizards and frogs; "Cave
  populations primarily prey on cockroaches", and in caves it "tend[s] to be the dominant predator".
- **Breeds:** the male leaves a packet of sperm on the ground for the female to pick up (mating lasts one to eight
  hours), and an egg sac develops. In the group, the young climb onto the mother's back after hatching and ride there
  until their next moult, and mother and young touch each other with their feelers.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) territorial: a dispute starts with waving the grabbing arms, and if that doesn't
  settle it they fight, and the loser is sometimes eaten; (2) it can drop a leg at a joint and regrow it, and a
  regrown feeler grows even longer; (3) blinded ones still find their way home, mostly by smell. ((2) and (3) are
  stated for the group, not this species.)
- **Danger to people:** harmless apart from the spiny grab (the group page).
- **Habitat and fit:** wet subtropical forest, caves, trees and big stumps; the Caribbean, Central America and the
  north of South America. Body under 2 cm, front legs nearly 25 cm. Fits the Centipede Cavern if it is warm and
  damp; a poorer fit for the cold caves.
- **Needs gone animals?** Only as an occasional prey (lizards, frogs). But its cave diet is mostly cockroaches,
  which the owner cut, so in the game it would hunt the cave flies, cave beetles and millipedes instead.
- **Adds:** a frightening-looking but harmless hunter whose long feelers sweep the dark, which fights its own kind
  and carries its young on its back: fear that turns into a lesson.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amblypygi ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrynus_longipes ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charinus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_diadema
- **Not confirmed:** a well-described cave-only (blind) species: Charinus troglobius and C. cavernicolus are cave
  species by name, but their pages are lists with no biology; whether P. longipes mothers carry young (stated for the
  group, not the species); how many young. Damon diadema also lives "in caves, crevices and under fallen logs" in
  East Africa, but its page gives no behaviour.

### 4b. Blind cave crayfish — the southern cave crayfish, Orconectes australis — fits the Underground River
- **What it is:** an "obligate cave crayfish" (it lives only in caves) of cave streams in Alabama and Tennessee; a
  1977 study calls it the "blind cave crayfish". Cave crayfish of its genus are "usually depigmented, often blind".
- **Eats:** not stated for this species. A close cave relative, the Benton County cave crayfish (Cambarus
  aculabrum), eats "detritus washed into the cave from the surface, and ... algae, fungus, and other organisms
  associated with deposits of bat guano"; the U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service says such crayfish "rely on outside
  sources of organic matter for food such as leaf litter, dead animals or bat guano".
- **Breeds:** slowly. A five-year study of more than 3,800 marked crayfish found females mature at 4–5 years and
  first breed at 5–6 years; it lives up to about 22 years (fewer than 5% live longer). The famous claim of more than
  176 years, from a single 1970s study, was far too high.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) slow: females first breed at 5–6 years, so a cave-crayfish farm is a long-term
  investment; (2) its cave relative keeps away from cave mouths, where surface hunters catch it, and needs clean, clear,
  well-oxygenated water; (3) another cave relative, the prickly cave crayfish (Cambarus hamulatus), glows under
  ultraviolet light, which a player's lamp could use.
- **Danger to people:** not stated.
- **Habitat and fit:** cave streams; the Benton County relative's cave water stays at about 14 °C (57 °F). Fits the
  Underground River.
- **Needs gone animals?** Partly: cave crayfish feed in part on bat guano, and bats are gone. Leaf litter and dead
  animals washed in from the surface remain, so it can live in 2126 caves, just on less food.
- **Adds:** a pale, blind, slow-growing crayfish that is a long-term investment rather than a quick harvest, and
  that needs clean water.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orconectes_australis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orconectes ;
  https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1111/j.1365-2427.2012.02812.x (Venarsky, Huryn and Benstead 2012, Freshwater
  Biology: abstract) ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambarus_aculabrum ;
  https://www.fws.gov/species/benton-county-cave-crayfish-cambarus-aculabrum ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambarus_hamulatus
- **Not confirmed:** what O. australis itself eats; its size; how many eggs; whether it pinches people; whether it
  glows under ultraviolet light itself (only shown for C. hamulatus); its own need for clean water (stated for its
  relative; a 1977 study title is about blind cave crayfish dying in chlorinated stream water).

## 5. Scorpion Rocks (dry, rocky desert; mining by day, venom, heat)

### 5a. Camel spider (sun spider) — Galeodes arabs — fits well
- **Eats:** "insects, small rodents and lizards"; its jaws can cut through the thin bones of small birds (species
  page). For the whole group, insects are "the bulk of their diet", plus spiders, scorpions and smaller sun spiders;
  small lizards, birds and mammals are taken too. Adult females often eat until they can't walk for a while; before
  laying, a 5 cm kind was seen in a laboratory to eat more than 100 flies.
- **Breeds:** the male turns the female on her back and passes sperm with his jaws; she fattens up, digs a burrow
  and lays 50–200 eggs there, and some kinds guard them until they hatch (group page; not stated for this species).
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) runs at up to 16 km/h, hunting by stalking, chasing and ambush, catching prey
  with suction pads on the tips of its feelers and chopping it with jaws a third of its body length; (2) it hides from
  the sun and hunts at night; (3) the old story that it chases people is false: it is "merely trying to stay in the
  shade/shadow provided by the human", so in a hot zone it would follow the player's shadow. Many rattle their jaws
  to make a sound.
- **Danger to people:** "not venomous, but their bite is painful"; the group page: "a painful nip, but nothing
  medically significant".
- **Habitat and fit:** North Africa and Western Asia (Egypt, Israel, Syria); the group lives in sand dunes, gravel
  plains and rocky hillsides. Up to about 15 cm long. Fits Scorpion Rocks, beside the North African fat-tailed scorpion.
- **Needs gone animals?** It eats lizards, rodents and birds when it can, but mostly insects and other arthropods, so
  it can live without them.
- **Adds:** a fast night hunter that eats scorpions and dashes after the player's shadow by day: a scare that turns
  out to be about shade, and a real check on the zone's scorpions.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galeodes_arabs ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solifugae
- **Not confirmed:** this species' breeding and egg count; whether it really follows people's shadows (stated for the
  group); how often it eats scorpions in the wild.

### 5b. Antlion — Euroleon nostras (better documented than Myrmeleon formicarius) — fits, in sheltered sandy pockets
- **Pit trap confirmed:** only three tribes of antlions dig pits; Euroleon nostras is one of the pit-diggers (the
  Myrmeleon formicarius page doesn't say). The larva walks backwards in a spiral, ploughing loose soil with its rear
  end, until it has a cone about 5 cm deep and 7.5 cm wide, then waits buried at the bottom. An insect that slips in
  is brought down by showers of sand the larva throws.
- **Does it need sand?** It needs loose soil, "sand being a common choice", in "dry places protected from the rain".
  Euroleon nostras larvae chose "dry sand under a rock overhang or other surface that prevented direct rainfall". So
  in a rocky desert it lives in sand pockets under ledges, not on bare rock.
- **Eats:** larvae eat small arthropods, mainly ants: hollow jaws inject venom and digestive juices, the larva sucks
  the body out and flicks the dry husk out of the pit. Adults of some kinds eat pollen and nectar, others small insects.
- **Breeds:** grows for up to two years (antlions can go many months without food), then pupates in a ball of sand
  bound with silk, buried several centimetres deep; the adult comes out about a month later and lives about 25–45 days,
  a weak flier out at night looking for a mate.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) the pit and the sand-throwing; (2) the "doodlebug" trails it leaves when it moves
  house, winding lines in the sand; (3) a long, slow larval life and a short adult one.
- **Danger to people:** not stated.
- **Habitat and fit:** antlions live in dry places, most kinds in warm regions. E. nostras: almost all of Europe,
  Morocco, Turkey and the Caucasus; adult about 30 mm, wingspan about 70 mm. Fits Scorpion Rocks: its content sheet
  has "gravel washes & dune pockets" and shady rock (`docs/product/economy/zones/scorpion_rocks.md`).
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds:** a trap the player can see and must step around, or bait with ants to watch it work; the zone's
  everyday hunter of the ants and small bugs that cross the sand.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antlion ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroleon_nostras ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrmeleon_formicarius
- **Not confirmed:** a true desert pit-builder (the Myrmeleon formicarius page has no biology, and no desert
  species page was found); whether antlions can trap anything as big as a person's foot (the game's giant scale).

### 5c. Fog-basking darkling beetle — Stenocara gracilipes fails; Onymacris unguicularis fits only a sandy, foggy place
- **The famous story is wrong for Stenocara:** a 2010 study (Nørgaard and Dacke) found that Stenocara gracilipes
  "never has been observed to actively fog-bask in nature"; in their tests "only O. unguicularis could be observed to
  actively collect water from the fog"; and the identity of the beetle in the well-known 2001 report "has been
  questioned" (other researchers name it as Physosterna cribripes). (The Wikipedia page on Stenocara still tells the
  old story.) Stenocara was collected "in a rocky side canyon", so it suits rocks, but it has no fog trick.
- **Onymacris unguicularis, the real fog-basker:** on foggy nights it climbs out of the sand onto a dune's slope and
  does a headstand with its back raised into the wind, so fog collects on its back and runs down to its mouth; some
  beetles drink 34% of their body weight. By day it is active and it buries itself in the sand at night.
- **Eats / breeds / danger:** not stated on the pages read.
- **Habitat and fit:** "almost entirely restricted" to the dune fields of the Namib Desert, whose water comes mainly
  as fog blowing in from the sea. Scorpion Rocks is mostly rock and baked clay inland, east of the river, with only
  "dune pockets" (its content sheet), and the game has fog only as planned future weather
  (`docs/product/architecture/architecture_weather.md`). Poor fit unless the zone gets real dunes and night fog.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds (if it fits):** a living water lesson, a beetle standing on its head at night to drink the fog, and an easy,
  harmless catch in a zone of deadly things.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stenocara_gracilipes ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onymacris_unguicularis ;
  https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2918599/ (Nørgaard and Dacke 2010, Frontiers in Zoology)
- **Not confirmed:** what either beetle eats; their size; a darkling beetle with a playable trick that lives on
  rock rather than sand (none was checked).

## 6. Shallow Swamp and Deep Swamp

### 6a. Fishing spider — the six-spotted fishing spider, Dolomedes triton (or the great raft spider, D. plantarius) — fits
- **Eats (D. triton):** water insects, small things living on the water's skin, land insects that fall in,
  tadpoles, frogs and small fish; it can catch "fish up to five times their body size". It rests with its front
  legs on the water, feeling for ripples, runs across the surface, and dives up to 18 cm for prey.
- **Breeds:** the female carries her egg sac in her jaws, then builds a "nursery web" over plants and guards it; the
  young stay under her guard until they scatter. Virgin females attack males in 20–30% of pairings.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) walks on water, to hunt and to escape; (2) dives when alarmed and can stay under
  for up to 90 minutes inside a coat of air, coming out dry; (3) the guarded nursery webs on swamp plants.
- **Danger to people:** not stated on the species or genus pages.
- **Habitat and fit:** ponds, lake shores, slow streams and their weedy edges, across North America. Female body
  15–20 mm, about 60 mm with legs. Fits the Shallow Swamp, and the Deep Swamp's edges.
- **The great raft spider (D. plantarius), Europe:** eats pond skaters, dragonfly larvae, smaller water spiders and
  small fish (sticklebacks), and tadpoles; hunts with its back legs on a stem and front legs on the water; the female
  carries her egg sac for about three weeks, dipping it in the water to keep it damp; males court on the water by
  tapping the surface and bobbing. It needs clean, standing or slow, non-acid water in fen and "grazing marsh", with
  plants standing out of the water, and avoids shaded water. Vulnerable (IUCN). Body just over 20 mm, 70 mm with legs.
- **Needs gone animals?** No: frogs and tadpoles are on the menu but it lives on insects and fish. (The great raft
  spider's prey includes pond skaters, which is the roster's water strider.)
- **Adds:** a spider that hunts the water's surface and dives out of the player's reach, guards nursery webs on swamp
  plants, and takes the fish players fish for.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomedes_triton ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomedes_plantarius ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomedes
- **Not confirmed:** whether either bites people or how badly; whether the great raft spider needs the grazing in
  "grazing marsh" (cattle are gone).

### 6b. Water scorpion — Nepa cinerea — fits the Shallow Swamp
- **Eats:** water fleas, water insect larvae, young small fish and tadpoles (German page); mostly water insects, only
  "occasionally" small fish or tadpoles (family page). It waits on water plants, in mud or on old leaves, snaps its
  front legs shut on prey, stabs it with its beak and sucks it out.
- **Breeds:** mates in spring; the female lays 10–20 eggs in rotting plants or mats of algae; each egg has about
  seven breathing threads that float in the water. The young grow through five stages, split by moults, to adult by
  September.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) breathes through a two-part snorkel about 10 mm long at its tail, which it keeps
  at the surface, so it lives where the water is shallow; (2) an ambush hunter that waits on plants, mud or old
  leaves, and "certainly looks like" a scorpion (Wildlife Trusts); (3) it has full wings but is seldom seen flying.
  It can live several years.
- **Danger to people:** "a painful bite (strictly speaking a sting by their pointed proboscis), but this is much less
  harmful to humans than a true scorpion's sting" (family page).
- **Habitat and fit:** shallow water near the banks of still or slow, muddy water: ponds, marshes, canals, small
  rivers; Europe, Africa and northern Asia. 17–25 mm plus the snorkel. A poor swimmer that crawls on plants. Fits the
  Shallow Swamp; in the Deep Swamp only in the shallows.
- **Needs gone animals?** No: tadpoles are occasional prey only.
- **Adds:** a hidden biter in the shallows that teaches players to watch where they wade; the tip of its snorkel at
  the surface could be the clue that gives it away.
- **Season note:** adults spend the winter on the bottom of muddy water; the game has no seasons.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepa_cinerea ; https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserskorpion ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepidae ; https://www.wildlifetrusts.org/wildlife-explorer/invertebrates/true-bugs/water-scorpion
- **Not confirmed:** its predators; whether it plays dead when handled.

### 6c. Dobsonfly — Corydalus cornutus — fails for the swamps (running water); fits the deep river instead
- **Habitat (the question):** running water. It lives "near the swift-flowing, unpolluted streams where its aquatic
  larvae develop"; the larvae hide under stones. Not a still-water swamp animal.
- **Eats:** the larvae (hellgrammites) eat soft-bodied water animals and "are among the top invertebrate predators
  in the streams in which they live"; adults are not thought to eat and usually die within a week.
- **Breeds:** eggs laid near the water's edge on a rock or overhanging leaves; the larvae hatch at night and drop or
  crawl into the stream; they grow for one to three years, shedding their skin 10–12 times, to 90 mm, then climb out,
  walk up to 15 m and dig a chamber in damp soil under a rock or log to pupate.
- **Behaviour worth playing:** (1) adults are night fliers drawn to lights; (2) the male's sickle-shaped jaws are up
  to 40 mm long, but "relatively harmless to humans", mainly for impressing females (order page); the female's short
  jaws are powerful; (3) the larvae "are used by anglers as bait".
- **Danger to people:** larvae can give "a painful bite" (order page).
- **Size:** adults up to 140 mm long, wingspan about 130 mm. Eastern North America.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Better use:** a fast, clean stream (the deep river, if it runs fast), where its larva is a top hunter under the stones and
  a fishing bait the player can dig for; lights at night would bring the adults in.
- **Still-water relative for the swamp slot — the alderfly, Sialis lutaria:** its larvae live "in mud and detritus
  under water" and hunt small invertebrates for one to two years; adults are bad fliers that live 2–3 days without
  eating; about 20 mm. Real, but little to play with.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corydalus_cornutus ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megaloptera ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corydalidae ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialis_lutaria ;
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauliodes
- **Not confirmed:** the fishflies (Chauliodes), the dobsonfly's relatives often said to live in still water: their
  page has no biology, so that could not be checked; whether the alderfly's mud is still or running water.

## 7. Deep Swamp apex predator (deep, still, plant-choked water)

With the giant water bug and the diving beetle cut, the strongest hunters left for weedy still water are dragonfly
nymphs and the big fishing spiders (my reading of the pages below; none of them ranks the hunters of such water).
Three options were checked.

### 7a. The emperor dragonfly's nymph — Anax imperator — the best fit, and no new species
- **What it is:** the young of the emperor dragonfly, which is already on the roster for the swamps. The bug table
  already treats a dragonfly nymph as "the dragonfly's own young", not another bug, so this is a role for the
  emperor's young, not a new species. Its nymphs are "very large–around 46 millimetres" and "very aggressive"; it
  breeds in "large ponds to dikes and slow-moving rivers, but require[s] a plentiful supply of vegetation in the
  water", and sometimes in brackish water.
- **Eats (dragonfly nymphs):** mostly water insect larvae, and larger ones "prey on tadpoles and small fish"; some
  Anax nymphs "have even been observed leaping out of the water to kill full-grown tree frogs".
- **Hunts:** an extendable lower lip armed with hooks and spines, folded under the body at rest, "struck out at
  great speed by hydraulic pressure"; it shoots off by squirting a jet of water from its rear, and breathes with gills
  inside its body.
- **Grows:** the nymph stage "lasts up to five years in large species", so the oldest are the biggest.
- **Danger to people:** not stated.
- **Needs gone animals?** No: tadpoles and frogs are extra prey, not needed.
- **Adds:** the swamp's real underwater terror, lurking in the weeds with a striking jaw and a jet escape, and the
  same species as the big blue dragonfly over the water, so a player sees its whole life in one zone.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_dragonfly ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragonfly
- **Not confirmed:** what emperor nymphs in particular eat (the prey facts are for dragonfly nymphs and the genus
  Anax); how long the emperor's own nymph stage lasts; a source calling it the top hunter of such water (the pages
  say only that it is "very aggressive" and that big nymphs take tadpoles and small fish).

### 7b. Okefenokee fishing spider — Dolomedes okefinokensis — best new species
- **What it is:** a fishing spider "associated with swampy areas", named after the Okefenokee Swamp; Georgia and
  Florida, perhaps Alabama and Louisiana. Females are about 30 mm long in the body (males about 8 mm), bigger than
  the six-spotted fishing spider (15–20 mm) and the great raft spider (just over 20 mm).
- **Eats:** "primarily ... aquatic insects, but also ... small vertebrates".
- **Hunting, breeding, danger:** not stated for this species. Its genus runs across the water, dives in a silvery
  coat of air, and the larger kinds catch small fish; the two fishing spiders in 6a guard nursery webs.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds:** the six-spotted fishing spider's heavier, swamp-only cousin: a player meets the smaller fishing spider in
  the shallows and this one in the deep swamp.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomedes_okefinokensis ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomedes
- **Not confirmed:** nearly all of its behaviour (the page is short); whether it eats fish.

### 7c. Diving bell spider — Argyroneta aquatica — a strong "diving" bug, but not an apex
- **Lives underwater:** it spins a sheet of silk between underwater plants and fills it with air carried down on the
  hairs of its body; oxygen seeps into the bell from the water, but the bubble slowly shrinks and must be topped up.
- **Eats:** water insects and crustaceans "such as mosquito larvae and Daphnia"; females wait in the bell and dart out
  at prey that touches it or its threads.
- **Breeds:** mates in the female's bell; she lays 30–70 eggs in a sac inside it. Males are almost 30% larger than
  females (10–15 mm), unusual for a spider.
- **Danger to people:** its bite "is often described as being very painful", with swelling, vomiting and slight fever
  lasting 5–10 days, but "solid evidence is lacking": the reports are old and unconfirmed.
- **Habitat and fit:** "clean freshwater habitats with aquatic vegetation": lakes, ponds, canals, marshes, slow
  streams; Europe to Siberia. Fish and frogs eat it, so it is not a top hunter.
- **Needs gone animals?** No.
- **Adds:** an air-bell home that mirrors the player's own diving gear, and a mosquito-larva eater that ties to the
  swamps' two mosquitoes; its feared bite fits "poison", but its need for clean water sits oddly in a zone of disease.
- **Sources:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_bell_spider
- **Not confirmed:** whether the bite is really dangerous.
- **Also checked, weaker:** Ancylometes, big South American fishing spiders (females to 5 cm body, 12 cm leg span;
  eat "anything from insects to small lizards and, occasionally, small fish"; nursery webs of over 100 young), but
  their page doesn't place them in swamps (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancylometes); and the water stick insect,
  Ranatra linearis (3–3.5 cm, ponds and marshes among water plants, can fly), whose page gives no hunting facts
  (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranatra_linearis).

## Ranked recommendation per zone

Weighed on real fit to the zone, nothing needed from the gone animals, play value, and the bug table's note that
zone bugs should be recognisable or variants of known ones, with now and then a unique one (silverfish row).

- **Bee Meadow:** (1) **European beewolf**: a real honey-bee hunter that "may greatly reduce" local honey bees,
  nests in sand like the zone's dune belt, and threatens a beekeeper at the flowers rather than at the hive.
  (2) **Hummingbird hawk-moth**: famous, harmless, with a daily round to learn; the day partner of the death's-head
  hawkmoth. Its caterpillars need bedstraw or madder. Not the bee-fly: its young need solitary bees, all cut; if a
  harmless look-alike is wanted, the bumblebee hoverfly fits better.
- **Ant Tunnels and Ant Colony:** (1) **Ant cricket** (inside the colony): a real thief that takes on the colony's
  smell and begs or scrapes food from the ants; it lives with Lasius ants, though the black garden ant itself isn't
  named. (2) **Silver-studded blue** (the half-outdoor Ant Tunnels): the black garden ant's named partner, trading
  sugar for protection; it may meet the same doubt that cut the aphids. Claviger testaceus is weaker: its main host is
  the yellow meadow ant.
- **Hilltop Meadow:** (1) **Large velvet ant, Mutilla europaea**, in place of the red velvet ant: it raids
  bumblebee nests (the zone's own bumblebee) and eats their honey; armoured, squeaking, a painful sting but not
  aggressive. The red velvet ant's hosts are cicada killers and horse guard wasps, not in the game. (2) **Mallophora
  ruficauda**, in place of the hornet robber fly (which needs cattle or rabbit dung) and the Promachus flies (pages
  with no biology): a real bee-killing robber fly that looks like a bumblebee; its young need chafer grubs in the soil.
- **Centipede Cavern and Underground River:** (1) **Southern cave crayfish** for the Underground River: a blind
  variant of a known bug that grows slowly (first breeds at 5–6 years, lives about 22); real cave crayfish eat partly
  bat guano, but leaf litter and dead animals remain. (2) **Whip spider, Phrynus longipes**, for the Centipede Cavern
  if it is warm and damp: frightening but harmless, the dominant hunter of its caves; its cave diet (cockroaches) would
  switch to the game's cave flies, beetles and millipedes.
- **Scorpion Rocks:** (1) **Camel spider, Galeodes arabs**: dry North Africa beside the fat-tailed scorpion; eats
  scorpions; seeks shade, so it chases the player's shadow; a painful bite but no venom. (2) **Antlion, Euroleon
  nostras**: pit traps confirmed; it needs dry, loose sand sheltered from rain, which the zone's dune pockets and
  ledges give. The fog-basking beetle does not fit: Stenocara doesn't fog-bask, and Onymacris needs dunes and sea fog.
- **Shallow and Deep Swamp:** (1) **Six-spotted fishing spider** for the Shallow Swamp: walks on water, dives for up
  to 90 minutes, catches fish, guards nursery webs. (2) **Water scorpion** for the shallows: an ambush biter that
  breathes through a tail snorkel. The dobsonfly fails here (fast streams only); it suits fast, clean water (the deep
  river, if it runs fast), where its larva is a top hunter and real fishing bait.
- **Deep Swamp apex:** (1) **The emperor dragonfly's nymph**: with the giant water bug and diving beetle gone, the
  strongest real hunter of weedy still water that is left, with a striking jaw and a jet escape; it needs no new
  species, as the emperor already flies over the swamps. (2) If a new species is wanted, the **Okefenokee fishing spider**, a bigger,
  swamp-only cousin of the Shallow Swamp's spider (little is known of its behaviour). The diving bell spider is the
  best "diving" bug, but it is not an apex and needs clean water.
