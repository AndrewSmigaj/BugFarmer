## 2. Per-game art-direction tells (most transferable first)

**Stardew Valley** — TOP-DOWN (3/4 oblique). Our closest analog.
- Tiny fixed grid (16×16) + heavy palette LIMITATION per sprite is the cohesion engine: "confined to a small grid... a limitation that can breed creativity" (mentalnerd). Every asset drawn to the same tiny budget = automatic family resemblance.
- Saturated color + STRONG OUTLINES do double duty: readability AND meaning — hair color, item quality, interactables all coded by hue/outline (anuflora). Outlines separate a busy foreground object from a busy ground tile.
- Caveat the community itself names: Stardew's art "feels like a mish-mash hobbled together" (Graveyard Keeper comparison, cbr/paste) — cohesion came from constraint + outline discipline, NOT from every sprite being individually gorgeous. Lesson for an AI pipeline: enforce the CONSTRAINT globally, don't chase per-sprite fidelity.

**Eastward** — TOP-DOWN (3/4). The richness benchmark for our view.
- Split-by-material construction: each object is authored in parts "according to its natural structure" so each material takes its own value/light — that's what makes surfaces read as brick vs tin vs cloth (gamedeveloper).
- Global LUT grade + fog beams at dawn/dusk unify a decayed, prop-dense world into one warm mood; "thousands of little touches" give density (80.lv). Warmth is deliberate: "unprecedented warmth... warm and fuzzy."
- Verticality/staircases solve top-down occlusion so you get depth without hiding gameplay (gamedeveloper).

**Moonlighter** — TOP-DOWN dungeon crawler. Direct mechanical analog.
- Light is FAKED by sprite superposition (layered glow sprites), not lit — a top-down-friendly, pipeline-agnostic way to get pooled light and focal emphasis (80.lv).
- Richness = brute detail investment "here and there," Ghibli-referenced, on an otherwise simple base (80.lv).

**Sea of Stars** — ISOMETRIC/near-top-down.
- Ambient life is the tell: animate "lights, fauna, and mechanisms," not just heroes — that's the "living place vs static diorama" difference (megavisions, gamedeveloper summary).
- Permits a wider-than-hardware palette + colored highlights — richness from MORE color used cohesively, not retro purity (megavisions).

**Spiritfarer** — SIDE-VIEW (platformer; layering/parallax is side-view).
- Interactables pop via "well-defined silhouettes" reading subtly against painted backgrounds — the transferable idea (silhouette-first readability) works in any view.
- Pastel band + Disney-warm temperature makes a death-themed game feel gentle: palette carries EMOTION, not just looks (medium, dualshockers).

**Hollow Knight** — SIDE-VIEW.
- Monotone, muted bluey-grey palette (near value-monochrome) is the cohesion device; the whole world is one value key, so a few saturated accents (and the white-ish knight) carry all the focus (Wikipedia/Ari Gibson).
- Hand-drawn sketches scanned straight to PNG give clean, consistent line — a single authored "hand" across everything. Parallax-heavy depth is side-view and won't transfer.

**Ori and the Blind Forest** — SIDE-VIEW.
- Hero = near-pure-white silhouette against hyper-detailed, high-chroma backgrounds so he's always instantly findable (Xbox Wire). Transferable rule: reserve your brightest value/highest chroma for the player + interactables; let the world be busy but LOWER contrast.
- Depth via 7,000 hand-painted layers in 3D space + custom fog/DoF — mostly side-view parallax, limited transfer to top-down.

**Dave the Diver** — SIDE-VIEW 2.5D.
- The tell is hybrid: pixel-art characters over low-poly smooth-textured 3D environments — deliberate texture CONTRAST (crunchy sprite vs soft 3D) makes both pop (gameworldobserver). Only partially transferable; the general lesson is intentional texture contrast between foreground actors and background.

**Graveyard Keeper** — TOP-DOWN (3/4).
- Praised as MORE cohesive than Stardew precisely because it commits to one dark, desaturated medieval palette — "the style is cohesive" vs Stardew's "mish-mash" (cbr/paste). Tone-locked palette = instant cohesion, even with an AI-ish asset mix.

**Coromon** — TOP-DOWN. Direct analog (monster-farming-adjacent).
- "Modernized" retro pixel: keep 16-bit charm but add modern lighting + smooth animation and VARIED, vibrant biomes (coromon.com/twinfinite). The tell is per-biome palette variety preventing sameness while a consistent sprite "hand" holds it together.

**Sun Haven** — TOP-DOWN. Direct analog (Stardew-like farm sim).
- Richness = clutter density: "a lot of little clutter that just looks so cute," super-detailed chibi props (review). Decoration density + a cute-consistent proportion system is the tell.

**Wylde Flowers** — 3/4 TOP-DOWN (but 3D-rendered, hand-painted textures).
- Not pixel art — cohesion comes from hand-painting stylized TILEABLE textures with a unified painterly hand (studiodrydock/supergenius). Transferable idea: consistency of the "brush/hand" matters more than the rendering tech.

## 3. Cross-cutting lessons — why these beat a flat AI-generated tileset

1. **A global grade/palette LUT is the cheapest cohesion the AI pipeline lacks.** AI sprites arrive with mismatched palettes/lighting; the richest-looking games (Eastward's LUT, Graveyard Keeper's tone-lock, Hollow Knight's monotone key) force EVERYTHING into one value key and temperature in post. Do this once over the whole top-down scene rather than per-sprite. (80.lv Eastward; cbr/paste; Ari Gibson/Wikipedia)

2. **Value/contrast is budgeted, not uniform: reserve the brightest, highest-chroma pixels for the player + interactables, keep the world busy-but-lower-contrast.** Ori's white silhouette and Spiritfarer's silhouette-pop are the same rule. A flat AI tileset fails because every tile shouts equally; nothing leads the eye. (Xbox Wire Ori; Spiritfarer medium)

3. **"1000 tiny motions" is what separates a living place from a diorama — and it's mostly AMBIENT PROPS, not characters.** Sea of Stars, Eastward, Moonlighter all invested in animating fauna, lights, mechanisms, and "little touches." This is fully top-down-compatible and is likely our biggest richness lever, since a static AI tileset has zero motion by default. (megavisions; gamedeveloper Eastward; 80.lv Moonlighter)

4. **Material readability comes from splitting objects by material and giving each its own value range** (Eastward's "natural structure" split + per-part bump/shading). Ask the generator for objects lit from ONE consistent angle with distinct material values, not a flatly-shaded blob — that's what makes brick read as brick next to tin. (gamedeveloper Eastward)

5. **Cohesion is bought with CONSTRAINT + a single "hand," not per-asset fidelity.** Stardew's tiny fixed grid, Hollow Knight's scan-one-artist line, Wylde Flowers' one painterly texture hand, Graveyard Keeper's tone-lock all beat "each asset individually gorgeous." For AI generation this means: pin resolution, palette, outline treatment, and light angle as hard global constraints, then vary palette PER BIOME (Coromon) and pile on prop clutter density (Sun Haven) for anti-repetition — cohesion from the constraint, richness from the density and per-biome variety.

Perspective caveats: parallax/multi-layer depth and DoF fog (Ori, Hollow Knight, Spiritfarer, Dave the Diver) are SIDE-VIEW techniques — they largely don't transfer to top-down; substitute Eastward-style verticality (staircases/height offsets) and layered fake-light sprites (Moonlighter) for depth instead.
