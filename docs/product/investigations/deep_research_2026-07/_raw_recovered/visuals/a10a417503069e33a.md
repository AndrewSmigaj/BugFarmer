## 2. Per-technique guidance (most impactful first)

### A. Tile variety / anti-repetition — the biggest lift for AI base tiles
The "samey grid" is the #1 tell of an AI-generated prototype, and it's the cheapest to fix.
1. **Weighted variant tiles.** Author a base material as a *set* of 3-6 interchangeable variants and have the builder resolve each cell to a variant by weight (e.g. 85% plain, 15% split among detail variants) ([Tilesetter](https://www.tilesetter.org/docs/tileset_behavior)). This alone kills the checkerboard.
2. **A scatter/decal layer over the base.** Separate the *ground* from the *scatter* (pebbles, tufts, wildflowers, cracks). Author it as a sparse overlay pass, not baked into the fill ([sprite-ai](https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/seamless-pixel-art-tiles), [Tilesetter](https://www.tilesetter.org/docs/tileset_behavior)).
3. **Autotiled transitions between materials.** Adopt a defined autotile contract — a **47-tile blob** set for organic masses (grass/dirt/water/sand), 16-tile only where blockiness is acceptable ([BorisTheBrave](https://www.boristhebrave.com/2021/11/14/classification-of-tilesets/)). Generate the edge/corner slots as sprites. Richness lives in the **edges** ([Lumisteria](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/10448)).
4. **Fix the AI generation itself.** Prompt for *evenly scattered detail* and *surface-not-scene* to defeat center-focus; prefer *organic noisy* surfaces to hide seams; **preview every tile in a 3×3 grid** before accepting ([sprite-ai](https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/seamless-pixel-art-tiles)).

### B. Environmental storytelling density / set-dressing
5. **Compose in vignettes, not scatter.** Each place implies a small past event and contrasts its neighbor ([LDB storytelling](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art/storytelling)). This is exactly our `place_*(b,ox,oy)` scene discipline — reinforce it.
6. **One hero prop + reused filler per vignette.** Give each scene a single focal object with the best art/placement; dress the rest with **reused props recolored/rotated/rescaled** ([Cuban Kitchen](https://80.lv/articles/cuban-kitchen-making-props-and-set-dressing-a-scene-with-a-story)). Cheap density, no new asset per item.
7. **Wear & tone variation = lived-in.** Layer dirt/damage and vary saturation; AI outputs a flat mono-hue, so generating **tone/wear variants** and placing worn vs clean deliberately is what sells history ([Cuban Kitchen](https://80.lv/articles/cuban-kitchen-making-props-and-set-dressing-a-scene-with-a-story)).
8. **Place for plausible use.** Props sit where a person would actually put/use them (reachable shelves, tools by the workbench). Plausibility > symmetry.

### C. Detail / focal hierarchy
9. **~70% calm, detail in clusters.** Keep most of the ground flat/negative and pack detail into *groups* with rest space between (Jim Brown GDC 2014; [gamedeveloper](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/composition-in-level-design)). Uniform clutter reads as noise, not richness — the opposite failure of the samey grid.
10. **Spend detail on the hero, not evenly.** Filler stays low-contrast so the hero prop/landmark wins the eye ([Cuban Kitchen](https://80.lv/articles/cuban-kitchen-making-props-and-set-dressing-a-scene-with-a-story), [LDB composition](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition)).

### D. Top-down composition
11. **Landmark by local contrast.** One memorable mass per zone that breaks the grid via **height, orientation (angle), or shape**, and is *useful* (interactable/destination) ([LDB composition](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition)).
12. **Don't chase "leading lines."** They're debunked; a screenshot isn't a level. Instead use **paths as structure + calm sightline corridors** toward the landmark, and let contrast do the pull ([LDB composition](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition)).
13. **Depth without a horizon:** frame with foliage/props at scene edges, overlap layers, vary tile value front-to-back — top-down depth comes from *overlap and value*, not perspective.

### E. Ambient micro-animation as a density lever
14. **Many cheap loops beat a few fancy ones.** 2-4-frame subtle loops (grass sway, flower nod, water shimmer, smoke, a 1-2px prop "breath") across *many* objects reads as a living world ([SLYNYRD](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/8/19/pixelblog-8-intro-to-animation)).
15. **Loop cleanliness > frame count.** One stray pixel in a loop is glaring; keep loops tight and seamless. Budget: animate the *ambient field* (water/foliage/smoke) first, then hero props, then occasional wandering critters/birds ([SLYNYRD](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/8/19/pixelblog-8-intro-to-animation)).

## 3. The 3-5 moves that most lift us from "samey prototype" to "rich"

1. **Ship a weighted-variant + scatter-layer tile system** (base variants by weight + a sparse pebble/tuft/flower overlay). Single biggest visual delta against the AI grid ([Tilesetter](https://www.tilesetter.org/docs/tileset_behavior), [sprite-ai](https://www.sprite-ai.art/blog/seamless-pixel-art-tiles)).
2. **Spend generation budget on transitions/edges + tone/wear variants,** not more flat fills — the eye lives at material boundaries and reads history from wear ([Lumisteria](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/10448), [Cuban Kitchen](https://80.lv/articles/cuban-kitchen-making-props-and-set-dressing-a-scene-with-a-story)).
3. **Authoring rule: every zone = a sequence of hero-prop vignettes over ~70% calm ground,** each vignette implying a small story and contrasting its neighbor. Reuse-recolor filler for density ([LDB storytelling](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/env-art/storytelling), [Cuban Kitchen](https://80.lv/articles/cuban-kitchen-making-props-and-set-dressing-a-scene-with-a-story), Jim Brown GDC 2014).
4. **One grid-breaking, useful landmark per zone** (height/angle/shape contrast + interactable) as the anchor ([LDB composition](https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition)).
5. **A pervasive cheap ambient-motion pass** — small clean loops on the water/foliage field and hero props, plus occasional critters — so the world reads as alive, not static ([SLYNYRD](https://www.slynyrd.com/blog/2018/8/19/pixelblog-8-intro-to-animation)).

**Pipeline hooks these imply:** a 3×3 tiling preview gate + scatter-detail prompt wording in the tile step; a weighted-variant + decal-layer resolver in the zone builder; a generation habit of producing tone/wear/rotation variants per prop; and a short-loop ambient-animation slot for tiles and select occupants.
