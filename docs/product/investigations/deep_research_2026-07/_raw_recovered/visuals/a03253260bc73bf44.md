## 1. The fog-of-war-MESH pattern (Edgar), concretely

**Mesh build & placement.** Instead of an image/post effect, Edgar places **one large mesh over the whole level** and makes it transparent where revealed. A "Create mesh" button spawns a `Fog Of War Mesh` GameObject (a pink placeholder quad in-editor; real material assigned at runtime), sized to cover the level, with a **Sorting Group** set so it draws on top of the world sprites. So the overlay is a single draw, one big quad, not per-cell geometry.

**The 1-texel-per-cell texture.** A CPU-side script tracks per-tile fog value in **[0,1]** (0 = fully hidden/dark, 1 = fully visible) and packs it into a texture where **each pixel = one tile**. It starts as a **1×1** texture and, when the first area is revealed, allocates a texture just big enough to bound all revealed tiles (plus buffer), reallocating when it outgrows. The shader reads this texture using the fragment's world/UV position mapped to cell coordinates.

**The shader lerp.** Edgar's own shader (`Edgar/FogOfWarMesh`, which must be added to *Always Included Shaders* for builds) samples that per-cell texture and, per the docs, **"lerps between the actual tile graphics and the fog colour"** based on the pixel's fog value. The docs do NOT publish the HLSL, but the operation is the canonical one confirmed by the other sources: `finalColor = lerp(fogColor, revealedColor, maskValue)` (and for an overlay you drive **alpha** instead of sampling the tile: `alpha = 1 - maskValue`, dark where hidden).

**Interaction with Light2D — the key question.** Edgar's stated reason for the mesh (vs an image effect) is precisely that the mesh **"interacts with the lighting system."** Mechanically: the fog mesh is just another object in the 2D sorting order drawn *on top of the already-lit world sprites*, so **the darkness sits AFTER lighting** — the lit scene shows through the transparent holes, and the fog color/darkness overwrites where hidden. Notably Edgar does **not** rely on a fancy shader-lighting handshake: its sample `MyFogOfWarCallback` **disables `Light2DBase` components on Awake and re-enables them in `OnRoomRevealing()`** — i.e. lights in unrevealed regions are simply turned off, and turned on as areas reveal. Transitions are CPU-side: when fog changes it re-uploads the texture to the GPU (texture *recreation*, not per-frame per-pixel, is the cost). Benchmark ceiling ~250k tiles / 500×500.

**Soft edges in Edgar** come from interpolation modes, not blur: **Smooth** (per-pixel interpolation between neighboring cell values), **Tile-based** (hard, granularity 1), and **Custom** with `TileGranularity` (subdivide each tile into N×N chunks) and `FogSmoothness` (step size = 1/FogSmoothness, e.g. 2 → values {0,.25,.5,.75,1}). No Gaussian blur; bilinear sampling of the low-res mask does the smoothing.

## 2. URP 2D blend styles — can a generated mask drive darkness?

Your `Renderer2D.asset` exposes four blend styles: **Multiply, Additive, Multiply with Mask (R), Additive with Mask (R)**. Semantics from the docs:

- **Blend factors:** Multiply = `Modulate 1 / Additive 0` → the lit result is `spriteColor * light`. Additive = `Modulate 0 / Additive 1` → light is added. (Subtractive = Additive −1.) So **Multiply is the darkening operator**: where a Multiply-style light texture is dark, sprites go dark.
- **The "with Mask" variants** use a **mask channel (R/G/B/A)** where **max value = full light, min = no light**. Crucially this mask is a **per-sprite secondary/mask map** (a Secondary Texture on the sprite that the light is affecting), controlling *where a given light is allowed to touch that sprite*. It is **not** a screen-space darkness field you paint globally. Also note: **each blend style you enable allocates another full light render texture** — a real memory/perf cost, so don't add a fifth style casually.

**So, can a generated per-cell mask drive darkness through a blend style?** Two honest answers:

1. **Via the "with Mask" blend styles: no, not as a global field.** Those masks are authored per-sprite (Sprite Editor secondary textures) and gate light on that specific sprite. Wiring a runtime-generated 160×160 world field into every world sprite's R-channel mask is impractical.

2. **Via a Sprite Light2D carrying a runtime texture: yes, and this is the clean URP-native route.** A **Sprite** Light2D "emits light in the shape of a sprite" and has a **Sprite** property. If you build a `Sprite.Create(...)` from your runtime `Texture2D` mask and feed it to a **Sprite Light2D on the Multiply blend style**, the light texture *is* your darkness field: bright texels light the world, dark/black texels multiply the world toward black. Because your world sprites are all `Sprite-Lit-Default`, they already receive this. This reuses your existing lighting composite (no extra full-screen mesh, no custom shader) and darkness lands **in the same pass as your Global + Lamp lights**. Caveat: it's a single quad-shaped sprite light, so it darkens within its rect, and you'd update the Sprite's texture as the player digs. This is arguably a better fit for your "cosmetic, client-side" darkness than a separate overlay mesh.

**Global Light day/night** stays as-is: your one **Global Light2D** modulates everything uniformly (no falloff, one per blend style per sorting layer); the darkness field is an *additional* Multiply contribution on top.

## 3. Generating + UPDATING the per-cell mask at runtime

Two mechanisms seen in real code:

- **CPU `Texture2D` + SetPixels/Apply** (Edgar-style, and the classic per-cell approach): allocate a `Texture2D(W, H, RGBA32 or R8, mipChain:false)`, `filterMode = Bilinear` (bilinear is what smooths the low-res cells for free), write cell values with `SetPixels32`, then `Apply(false)` to upload. For your **160×160 window that's 25,600 texels** — a full `SetPixels32`+`Apply` on 25.6k pixels is **sub-millisecond**, and the GPU upload of a 160×160 RGBA32 texture is ~100 KB (R8 = 25 KB), trivial. When the player digs, you don't need a full rebuild: **write only the dirty cells** (`SetPixels`/`SetPixels32` into a small dirty rect, or `Graphics.CopyTexture`/partial `Apply`), so a dig touches a handful of texels — effectively free.

- **RenderTexture + camera/blit** (metaphore-style): `FowEffectController.cs` uses `RenderTexture` ping-pong (`textureIn`/`textureOut`), sized to the **camera window** not the world (`bufferWidth = CeilToInt(camSize.x * ppu)`, ppu=16), and runs an FxChain material to produce cloudy edges. This keeps textures small and moves smoothing to the GPU. Use this if you want the blur/noise done on-GPU every frame; use the SetPixels path if your data changes rarely (digging) and you want zero per-frame GPU work.

**Cost verdict for 160×160:** negligible. Full rebuild <1 ms CPU + ~100 KB upload; incremental dig updates are microseconds. This is not your bottleneck.

## 4. Soft edges: blur vs Perlin-jittered boundary (real shader code)

A raw 1-texel-per-cell mask is blocky. Three proven softening layers, cheapest first:

1. **Bilinear filtering** of the low-res mask (Edgar, andrewhung). Free, but only linear ramps — still reads as "cells."
2. **Temporal `lerp`** for reveal/hide fades (andrewhung): `col.a = lerp(aPrev, aCurr, _Blend)` with `_Blend` animated 0→1 over frames. Smooths *transitions in time*, not the spatial boundary.
3. **Perlin-noise boundary jitter (the organic look you want)** — from Lexdev's real Civ VI shader. The boundary is displaced by world-position noise so it dissolves instead of stair-stepping:

```glsl
// distort the mask value with world-space noise so the edge is organic
float noise     = tex2D(_Noise, IN.worldPos.xz / _MapSize).r;
float maskNoise = clamp(maskVal - pow(1.0 - maskVal, 0.01) * noise, 0, 1);

// only the boundary is affected; interior stays fully lit/fully dark
if (maskNoise < _Cutoff)
    tile = lerp(_MapColor * tileMap * mapBackground, _MapEdgeColor, maskNoise / _Cutoff);
```

The `(1 - maskVal)` weighting confines the jitter to boundary texels; interior fully-dark/fully-lit cells are untouched. Their per-cell writer also uses `smoothstep(_Radius + _Blend, _Radius, dist)` to feather each cell's contribution. Porting to your case: sample your 160×160 mask, sample a tiling noise texture by **world position** (so the pattern is stable as the camera moves), and `smoothstep`/`clamp` the two together to get an organic dark boundary. A cheap alternative to a separate noise pass is a small **Gaussian/box blur of the mask** (3–5 taps) at generation time, but noise-by-world-position gives the non-blocky *organic underground* silhouette you're after.

## 5. Shadow Caster 2D — can wall occupants cast real cast-shadows?

**Yes, mechanically.** Add a **Shadow Caster 2D** to each solid occupant; its shape defaults to the sprite outline or can be edited via the **Shape Editor**. For it to do anything, your **Lamp `Light2D`s must have Shadows enabled** (Shadow Strength 0–1, Softness, Falloff). The **Global** light can't cast shadows (no shadow props on Global), which is fine — day/night shouldn't cast. So Lamp/player point lights getting occluded by walls is fully supported and separate from your cosmetic darkness mask.

**Feasibility/perf on many occupants — this is the catch:**

- URP renders **shadow geometry into a shadow texture per shadow-casting light, per frame** (stage 1 of the 3-stage 2D pipeline). Cost scales with **(number of shadow-casting lights) × (number/complexity of shadow casters in range)**. Hundreds of individual `ShadowCaster2D` occupants around several lamps is the expensive case.
- **Use `Composite Shadow Caster 2D`** on a parent to merge many child casters into one shadow shape — this is the intended path for tilemap-like walls and cuts per-caster overhead. Tools like [CraftedPvP/Unity-URP-Shadow-Caster-Tilemap](https://github.com/CraftedPvP/Unity-URP-Shadow-Caster-Tilemap) / the [Jailbroken generator script](https://jailbrokengame.com/2023/05/08/script-for-generating-shadowcaster2ds-for-tilemaps-repost/) auto-build shadow polygons wrapping the tilemap collider outline.
- **Critical gotcha (multiple sources):** **closed** loops break point-light shadows. A ring/enclosed tilemap collider produces two overlapping convex meshes that *enclose the point light*, and the shadow renderer then produces garbage inside. Workaround: leave a one-tile gap in walls (fill it on another layer), or generate open contours. For a fully enclosed underground this is a real design constraint.

**Recommendation for your case:** cast-shadows from every dug wall block, updated as the player digs, means regenerating composite shadow geometry on every dig around every nearby lamp — heavier and fiddlier than your cosmetic mask. A pragmatic split: keep the **data-driven darkness mask** as the primary "you can't see into unmined rock" effect (cheap, exact shape), and only add **Shadow Caster 2D on a limited set of prominent occupants near lamps** if you want real occlusion flair — not on the entire mined field.

## 6. PERF: full-screen multiply mesh + runtime mask on a 160×160 window

- **The overlay mesh** is **one quad, one draw call**, full-screen. Fill-rate for a single screen-covering transparent quad is negligible on any GPU that runs URP.
- **The mask update** (Section 3): full rebuild <1 ms CPU + ~100 KB upload; incremental dig updates ≈ microseconds. Not a bottleneck.
- **Blend-style cost:** if you route darkness through a **Sprite Light2D on an existing blend style**, you add **zero** new render textures. If you instead enable a *new* blend style (e.g. a dedicated Multiply-with-Mask), the docs warn **each blend style allocates another full-screen light render texture** — that's the main hidden cost, plus `Render Texture Scale` lets you trade quality for memory.
- **Net:** the darkness overlay/mask is cheap and dominated by, at most, one extra light render texture. The **expensive knob is Shadow Caster 2D** (per-light shadow passes), not the mask.

### Bottom line for your constraints
Given URP17 2D, all world sprites on `Sprite-Lit-Default`, one Global + Lamp `Light2D`, and darkness being cosmetic/client-side, the lowest-friction path is a **runtime-updated per-cell `Texture2D` mask (1 texel = 1 cell, bilinear, R8) driven either into a single Multiply overlay quad drawn on top (darkness AFTER lighting) OR into a Sprite Light2D on the Multiply blend style (darkness composited WITH your lights)**. Add **world-position Perlin noise + smoothstep** (Lexdev recipe) for the organic, non-blocky edge. Reserve **Shadow Caster 2D** for a few hero occupants near lamps, mind the **closed-loop shadow bug**, and use **Composite Shadow Caster 2D** if you go wider.
