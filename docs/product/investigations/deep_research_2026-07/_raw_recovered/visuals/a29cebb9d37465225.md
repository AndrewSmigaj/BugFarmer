# Look-and-Feel Research: Ambient Particles + Foliage/Water Motion for a Cozy Top-Down Pixel Farming Game

**Scope:** Two categories — (A) environmental/ambient particles, (B) foliage & water motion. Lighting excluded. Target: Unity 6 / URP 17.2, 2D Renderer, hard pixel art ~16px logical, gpt-image-1 raster sprites (single-frame, so runtime motion is attractive — you don't hand-author sway frames).

**Deep-read sources (full-fetch):** 12. **Real implementations studied:** Cyanilux 2D Water shader breakdown (node-level), VOiD1 free URP 2D wind shader asset, prime31 open 2D foliage (mesh-vert + spring), Daniel Ilett grass/rain breakdowns, NedMakesGames foliage shader. **Techniques cataloged:** 11 (6 particles + 5 motion) — over the ≥6 / ≥3-each quota.

## Bottom line — where to spend

**Cheap high-impact wins (do first):**
1. Ambient dust motes / drifting spores (one particle system per zone)
2. Wind sway on grass/crops/trees (single shader, applied to every foliage sprite)
3. Harvest/crop-pop particle bursts (gameplay juice)
4. Fireflies at dusk (particle system + optional glow)

**Medium polish (do next):**
5. Falling leaves/petals (seasonal signal), 6. Snow (reuses leaf system), 7. Player-parts-the-grass displacement, 8. Water flow + shoreline ripple, 9. Reeds/lilypad bob (reuses wind system)

**Expensive polish (do only if the scene needs it):**
10. Render-texture water reflections, 11. Full weather (collision-spawned rain splashes at scale + puddle-ripple shader), waterfall foam.

# CATEGORY A — Ambient / weather particles

### A1. Ambient dust motes / floating spores  *(CHEAP HIGH-IMPACT WIN)*
- **Approach:** One Unity Particle System (Shuriken) per zone, camera-anchored or world-space. Very low emission (~5–20 alive), large lifetime, near-zero gravity, tiny 1–2px sprites, slow upward/random drift. Add the **Noise module** (low strength, low frequency) for organic wander, and **Color-over-Lifetime** fading alpha in/out so motes twinkle rather than pop. For "catching the light," bias emission near where shafts fall. Curl-noise ([edom18 repo](https://github.com/edom18/CurlNoiseParticleSystem)) upgrades the drift to look non-repeating.
- **Cost:** Dev ~1–2 hrs for a reusable prefab. Runtime negligible (dozens of particles).
- **Reference game:** GameDev.net community wisdom explicitly cites windblown dust + swooping fireflies as the cheapest way to make static 2D scenes feel alive; Spiritfarer's hazy interiors lean on this constantly.
- **Fit:** **High.** Biggest atmosphere-per-hour ratio; sells "cozy" instantly indoors and in forests.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Sub-pixel drift of a 1px sprite shimmers/crawls. Either (a) let motes live as a soft over-grid layer (many cozy pixel games deliberately render VFX above the pixel grid), or (b) snap positions to the logical pixel grid. Continuous per-particle rotation of a pixel dot is fine only if it's a symmetric dot; rotating a shaped mote needs a flipbook, not live rotation.

### A2. Wind-driven falling leaves / petals  *(seasonal, cheap)*
- **Approach:** Particle System with **Emission** across the top of the screen, **Shape** = wide edge/box, **Noise module** for tumble, **Force-over-Lifetime** on X for a wind bias (tutorial uses X≈-4), **Rotation-over-Lifetime** for spin, **Texture Sheet Animation** for a flutter flipbook, **Size/Color-over-Lifetime** to fade at ground. Swap the leaf sprite per season (green→amber→bare, pink petals for a blossom event).
- **Cost:** ~2–3 hrs; runtime cheap (tens–low-hundreds of particles).
- **Reference game:** Ori's opening "leaf tumbling in the wind" (root-motion driven, per GDC notes) is the gold standard for organic tumble; Stardew's fall trees shed leaves on a chance timer.
- **Fit:** **High/Med.** Strongest cheap "season changed" signal; pairs with the wind shader (A/B synergy).
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** **Live rotation is the trap** — a rotating pixel leaf produces off-grid, non-pixel edges. Use a **pre-rendered rotation flipbook** (8–16 angle frames) instead of `Rotation-over-Lifetime`, OR accept leaves as a higher-res overlay layer. Keep leaf sprites at native art resolution.

### A3. Harvest / crop-pop bursts (gameplay juice)  *(CHEAP HIGH-IMPACT WIN)*
- **Approach:** A one-shot **burst emitter** fired on the harvest/chop/pickup event. Config from the game-feel breakdown: burst of 5–15 particles, donut/hemisphere shape, short life (0.5–1.5s), **size decreasing over lifetime**, slight outward speed + gravity. Skin it as leaf flecks, pollen puff, or sparkle. Layer with a 1-frame squash on the crop sprite and a tiny screenshake for maximum "juice."
- **Cost:** ~1–2 hrs for a parametric prefab reused across all harvest events. Runtime trivial (bursts are transient).
- **Reference game:** **Forager** (constant satisfying pop bursts on every gather) and **Celeste** (land/dash dust) are the canonical juice references; Sun Haven's "snappy crops" bounce when watered is the same feedback philosophy via sprite-anim.
- **Fit:** **High.** Directly reinforces the core farming loop; disproportionate perceived polish.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Keep flecks as tiny native-res sprites; avoid soft additive glow unless you commit to a glow overlay pass. Snap the burst origin to the tile grid so it reads as "from that crop."

### A4. Fireflies-as-particles (dusk/night mood)  *(cheap, high mood)*
- **Approach:** Particle System, low emission (10–40), **Noise module** for wandering flight, **Color-over-Lifetime** pulsing alpha for the blink. Two glow options: (a) attach the **Light module** with *Alpha Affects Intensity* so each mote's 2D light fades with its blink (Unity Learn technique) — but that's the "lighting" system you're covering separately, so for pure look-and-feel use (b) a soft additive bloom sprite on an overlay layer.
- **Cost:** ~2 hrs. Runtime cheap unless you attach per-particle lights (then it's a light-count budget question).
- **Reference game:** Ori's glowing forest ambient life; Spiritfarer's nighttime shimmer.
- **Fit:** **High** for evening scenes / specific biomes; gate to night so it stays special.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Soft bloom directly contradicts hard pixels. Decide up front whether the glow is an in-world pixel sprite (crisp) or a post/overlay bloom (soft). Mixing carelessly makes fireflies look like they're from a different game than the ground.

### A5. Rain + splashes + puddle ripples  *(weather; rain cheap, splashes = polish)*
- **Approach (three tiers):**
  - *Falling rain (cheap):* Shuriken with **Stretched Billboard** render mode, `Start Speed 0` + `Gravity Modifier 1`, **Simulation Space = Global** (so it doesn't ride the camera), ~2,500 emission. GPU **VFX Graph** version (1,500/s) scales to far more.
  - *Splashes (polish):* VFX-Graph path — `Collide with AABox` at ground level → `Trigger Event On Die` → splash sub-emitter (short-life, 3-frame flipbook). Shuriken can do the same with Collision + Sub-Emitter modules.
  - *Puddle ripples (cheap alternative):* **Don't** spawn a ripple particle per drop. Cyanilux/Ilett both recommend sampling a **flipbook normal-map texture inside the puddle/water shader** (3×3 = 9 frames), masked by a puddle map, converted via Normal-From-Height. Much cheaper and no z-fighting.
- **Cost:** Rain layer ~2–3 hrs. Collision splashes + puddle shader ~1–2 days. Runtime: rain cheap; collision splashes at scale are the expensive part (favor the flipbook route).
- **Reference game:** Stardew/Coral Island rainy days as a whole-mood shift. [Pixel Weather FX](https://forum.unity.com/threads/pixel-weather-fx-2d-and-3d-weather-particle-systems-in-the-pixel-art-style.315848/) sells 30 ready pixel prefabs if you want to skip authoring.
- **Fit:** **Med/High.** Weather variety is a cozy-farming staple, but it's a system, not a one-off. Ship rain first; add splashes/ripples as polish.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Stretched-billboard rain streaks are inherently sub-pixel diagonal lines — they'll anti-alias/shimmer against crisp tiles. Author rain as pixel-art streak sprites (or use the pixel weather pack) rather than thin billboards, and keep splash flipbooks at native res.

### A6. Snow (seasonal reuse)
- **Approach:** Clone the falling-leaves system: slower fall, gentle horizontal Noise sway, no rotation flipbook needed (round flakes), higher count, soft accumulation faked by a ground overlay tint/tilemap swap. Cheapest possible because it's a re-skin of A2.
- **Cost:** ~1 hr once A2 exists. Runtime cheap.
- **Reference game:** Stardew winter; Spiritfarer snowy regions.
- **Fit:** **Med.** High value for a winter season, near-zero marginal cost if you built leaves first.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** A 1px round flake is safe (rotation-invariant); just watch sub-pixel drift crawl — snap or accept the overlay-layer look.

# CATEGORY B — Foliage & water motion

### B1. Wind sway on grass / crops / trees  *(THE core "alive" upgrade — CHEAP HIGH-IMPACT WIN)*
- **Approach:** A single **Sprite/Unlit Shader Graph** applied to every foliage sprite that displaces vertices horizontally by a noise-driven wind vector, **anchored at the base**:
  - Sample a scrolling noise (seamless texture or `Simple/Gradient Noise` node) using **world-space position** so neighboring plants aren't in phase; animate by adding `Time × frequency` to the UV.
  - Scale the displacement by the sprite's **UV-Y coordinate** (0 at root, 1 at tip) so the base stays planted and only the top sways — this is the single most important trick for believable grass.
  - Layer two noise channels (R = primary directional sway, G = perpendicular turbulence/gust) for non-mechanical motion (NedMakesGames). In the vertex stage you must sample via `tex2Dlod`.
  - Global wind direction/strength as material or global shader properties so a "gust" can sweep the whole farm at once. A free drop-in exists: [VOiD1's URP 2D Wind Shader](https://void1gaming.itch.io/free-2d-wind-shader-for-unity-urp) (sprite sway + flag flutter, 27kB) to start same-day.
  - **CPU alternative (Stardew's actual method):** rather than a shader, skew each foliage sprite's top vertices at draw time by a shared wind sine — no shader, batches fine, but you touch every sprite in code. Best if your renderer is already custom; the shader is cleaner in Unity.
- **Cost:** ~half-day to a day for a robust graph (or minutes with VOiD1). Runtime **cheap** — it's a vertex-stage offset, no extra draw calls; applies to unlimited sprites sharing the material.
- **Reference game:** **Stardew Valley** — vanilla grass tufts and tree canopies do a subtle top-skew sway and leaf-rustle (the pronounced whole-field windswept look is the "Wind Effects" mod, confirming the base is deliberately gentle). This is exactly the "vertex-displacement driven by a scrolling noise, cheap at runtime" pattern.
- **Fit:** **High — do this first in Category B.** With gpt-image-1 single-frame sprites you get motion for free without hand-animating; one material lights up your entire flora library.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** **#1 gotcha of the whole report — pixel crawl/shimmer.** Sub-pixel horizontal displacement makes crisp pixels jitter between columns. Fixes: (a) **quantize the offset to whole logical pixels** (Posterize/floor the displacement so it moves in 1px steps), (b) keep amplitude to a few logical pixels, (c) if using Pixel-Perfect Camera, make sure the shader runs at logical resolution not screen resolution. Un-quantized wind on 16px art looks "wet"/mushy — quantized wind looks intentionally pixel-animated.

### B2. Player-parts-the-grass / crop walk-through displacement  *(tactile, High/Med)*
- **Approach — two families:**
  - *2D per-quad mesh (best for discrete grass tufts, prime31/LordNed):* each grass sprite is a quad; a trigger collider detects the player crossing its midpoint and offsets the **top two verts** horizontally (`verts[1].x = 0.5 + offset × BEND_FACTOR`), scaled by player distance and direction. On exit, a **spring system** pushes back (`force opposite current bend`) and oscillates until acceleration < ~5e-5, then **sleeps** to save CPU. Same code adds the ambient sine wind when idle, and a "part where you land" jump reaction. Deliberately done on the CPU mesh (not a vertex shader) to preserve Unity's sprite batching.
  - *Displacement-texture (best for dense fields, Zucconi/Ilett):* render "displacer" objects (the player) into a small **RenderTexture** each frame; the wind shader samples it to bend blades outward radially near the player. Passing raw player position works but "only for a few characters"; the texture route scales to crowds and can leave fading trails/trample marks.
- **Cost:** Per-quad: ~half-day, cheap runtime (sleeps when idle). Displacement-texture: ~1–2 days (extra camera/RT + shader), medium runtime.
- **Reference game:** **Ori** (character lushly parts foliage as it moves) is the aspirational feel; the prime31/LordNed 2D implementations are the concrete, ready-to-adapt version for a top-down sprite game.
- **Fit:** **High/Med.** Huge tactile payoff walking through your farm; start with per-quad on grass tufts (cheap), reserve the texture method for dense meadow zones.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Same crawl risk as B1 during the spring rebound — quantize the bend to whole pixels, and cap max bend so blades don't smear. In top-down, "part" means a small left/right lean of the tuft top, not a full flatten.

### B3. Water surface animation — flow + shoreline ripple + reflection  *(Med/High; reflection = expensive)*
- **Approach (Cyanilux 2D Water, studied node-level):**
  - *Flow/distortion (cheap):* two seamless noise textures (value + gradient) sampled from **world position** (not UV0, so it tiles seamlessly across a tilemap), each scrolled in a different direction via **Tiling-And-Offset + Time × direction vector**, speeds kept **< 0.1**; subtract 0.5 to center, add together, multiply by ~0.05 = distortion strength, put in a Vector2 with **Y=0** (preserve vertical sprite attachment), add to sampling UVs.
  - *Shoreline ripples (cheap):* a scrolling **Sine** wave (speed × frequency multipliers) fading as it travels inward from the shore; a **Square SDF** (grid dims as a Vector2, e.g. (12,4)) + Fraction computes distance-to-tile-edge so ripples don't bleed across tiles.
  - *Specular sparkle:* reuse the scrolling noise through a **Step** node (bright highlights where noise > threshold) + a subtle Smoothstep(0,0.5)×0.15.
  - *Reflections (expensive, optional):* a **second camera** parented to the main camera renders a "Reflection" layer into a **RenderTexture**, sampled with the distortion offset added to Screen Position; Lerp between the reflection and a cyan tint by the tint's alpha.
- **Cost:** Flow + shoreline + sparkle ~half-to-one day, cheap runtime. Reflection adds a full extra scene render (2nd camera) — the expensive part; skip unless a hero pond needs it.
- **Reference game:** **Spiritfarer's** reflective water (the Everdoor "half-circle bridge reflecting on water") is the reflection aspiration — though Spiritfarer's is hand-crafted/atmospheric, not a cheap shader, so treat reflections as premium.
- **Fit:** **Med/High.** Ponds/rivers are common in farming games; ship flow + shoreline ripple (cheap, big) and treat reflection as a hero-scene splurge.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Distortion UV offsets shimmer at sub-pixel scale. Cyanilux's fix: **sample noise with pixelated UVs** via a **Posterize** node (`floor(UV×Steps)/Steps`) or just render water at logical resolution under a **Pixel-Perfect Camera** and let upscaling quantize it. Un-posterized water looks smooth/HD and fights 16px art.

### B4. Reeds / lilypad / tall-crop bob  *(Med; near-free once B1 exists)*
- **Approach:** Two cheap variants: (a) reeds/tall crops reuse the **B1 wind shader** with higher amplitude and a slower frequency; (b) lilypads/floating objects get a slow **vertical sine bob** + tiny rotation in the same shader (offset phase per-object by world position so they don't bob in lockstep). Essentially free additional parameters on the existing wind material.
- **Cost:** ~1–2 hrs once B1 exists; runtime negligible.
- **Reference game:** Stardew's swaying grass/reeds near water; generic pond-life ambiance.
- **Fit:** **Med.** Cheap way to make water edges feel populated once you've paid for the wind shader.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Vertical bob must be **quantized to whole pixels** or floating pads jitter; keep amplitude to 1–2 logical pixels.

### B5. Waterfall foam / flowing falls  *(Low/Med; niche)*
- **Approach (Cyanilux waterfall):** flow via **scrolling Sine-offset UVs** oriented along the tangent (rotates with the object) — explicitly *no vertex displacement*, mesh stays low-poly. Foam in two zones: **stepped SDF** for hard-edged side outlines, and **smoothstep + scrolling-noise masks** at the top lip and bottom pool. Use **alpha-clip** (hard cutoffs, not soft transparency) for the stylized/pixel look.
- **Cost:** ~half-day; niche runtime cost; only worth it if you actually have waterfalls.
- **Reference game:** Generic stylized-waterfall shaders; pairs with a bottom-of-fall foam particle burst (mist).
- **Fit:** **Low/Med.** Do only if a specific scene features a fall; not core to farming.
- **Pixel-art gotcha:** Alpha-clip is your friend here — it keeps hard pixel edges. Continuous UV scroll should be pixel-quantized or the foam "boils" at sub-pixel resolution.

## Cross-cutting notes for Unity 6 / URP 17.2 2D + hard pixel art

- **The universal pixel-art rule:** every technique that *moves* pixels (wind sway, water distortion, bob, drift) must **quantize motion to whole logical pixels** (Posterize node in shaders, position-snap for particles) or render at logical resolution under the **2D Pixel-Perfect Camera** and let upscaling do the quantizing. Un-quantized motion is the single thing that makes pixel-art motion look "wet." This is confirmed explicitly by Cyanilux for water and is the same root cause for wind crawl.
- **VFX layering decision:** decide once whether particles live *on the pixel grid* (crisp, snapped, native-res sprites) or *above it* as a softer overlay (many cozy pixel games — and Spiritfarer's whole hand-drawn approach — deliberately float soft ambient VFX above crisp ground). Mixing per-effect without a rule is what makes a scene look inconsistent.
- **Rotation is the particle trap:** never use live `Rotation-over-Lifetime` on shaped pixel sprites (leaves, petals) — use pre-rendered rotation flipbooks. Round dots (dust, snow, sparkles) are safe to move but still need position snapping.
- **gpt-image-1 synergy:** because your sprites are single-frame raster, the **shader/particle route is strictly better than hand-animated sway** — one wind material animates your entire generated flora library for free; you never round-trip to the generator for motion frames.
- **Shuriken vs VFX Graph:** ambient/harvest/leaf/snow systems are all fine on classic Shuriken (CPU, hundreds of particles). Only reach for GPU **VFX Graph** if you want dense rain with collision splashes at scale.
- **Batching caution (from prime31):** doing grass wind on a *vertex shader* can break Unity sprite batching; the 2D-proven route for interactive grass is CPU mesh-vert manipulation with a sleep optimization. For non-interactive wind, a shared shader material batches fine.

## Suggested build order
1. Wind sway shader (B1) + ambient dust motes (A1) — one afternoon, transforms the whole game's feel.
2. Harvest bursts (A3) — hooks into your core loop, an hour of work.
3. Fireflies (A4) gated to night; falling leaves (A2) gated to autumn.
4. Player-parts-grass per-quad (B2) on grass tufts.
5. Water flow + shoreline ripple (B3, skip reflections) for ponds; reeds/lilypad bob (B4) reusing B1.
6. Rain layer (A5 tier 1) → snow reskin (A6). Add splashes/puddle-ripple flipbook and water reflections only for hero scenes (expensive polish).
