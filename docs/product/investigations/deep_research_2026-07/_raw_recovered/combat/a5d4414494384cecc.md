### SOURCE TABLE

**ROW 1 — https://gdkeys.com/keys-to-combat-design-1-anatomy-of-an-attack/** (GDKeys, "Anatomy of an Attack")
- **Technique:** 3-phase model — Anticipation (wind-up = the telegraph/"Sign" a specific attack is coming), Attack (active contact), Recovery (follow-through = the "Window of Opportunity"/WoO to counter, heal, reposition). Each attack needs a UNIQUE, "drastically different" wind-up so players tell them apart mid-fight. Recovery length is a design DIAL sized to the counterplay you want to grant.
- **Numbers:** Reaction time = 0.25s = 8f @30fps / ~15f @60fps. Anticipation = reaction + player-action trigger + difficulty buffer (e.g. 8f reaction + 10f block = 18f minimum wind-up). Hollow Knight Hornet worked example: Dash 15f/10f/10f (recovery too short to punish); Throw 20f/35f/10f; Lasso 20f/30f/LONG recovery "to let the player finish healing." Input-lag target <100ms (Meat Boy/Hollow Knight) vs ~160ms (GTA4/BF3, feels sluggish).
- **Map to bug game:** Every swarm attack gets the three beats. A swarm lunge/bite gets a visible wind-up (bugs rear back / cluster tightens) lasting ≥15f (~250ms) so sword/spear/net players can react. Tune each attack's RECOVERY as the deterministic punish window — heavy lunge = long recovery (free swing lands); light nip = fast recovery, unpunishable. Keep phase durations as authored integer frames; keep player input lag <100ms.

**ROW 2 — https://verynicegame.com/fighting/how-frame-data-works-in-fighting-games/** (frame-data explainer, used in place of the JS-only infil glossary)
- **Technique:** Startup = wind-up frames to first hit ("5f startup hits on frame 5; lower wins neutral"). Active = frames the hitbox connects ("more frames = longer reach in TIME"; notation 3(3)3 = active/gap/active). Recovery = the vulnerable tail: "You cannot act until it ends. Whiffed moves leave you open longest." Frame advantage: 0/plus on block = safe, minus = unsafe; recovery IS the punish window ("Minus 5 on block? Opponent punishes with a 4-frame jab"). A missed (whiffed) move exposes the FULL recovery = the biggest punish.
- **Numbers:** 60fps ⇒ 1 frame ~16.7ms. Light/fast attacks ~4-5f startup. SF6 Ryu MP = +2 on block (safe). A 4-frame jab is the canonical fast punish.
- **Map to bug game:** The counterplay math for the player's own weapons — NET: fast startup, wide/long active (catches many bugs), modest recovery; SPEAR: slow startup, narrow long active, long recovery (whiff = punished by the swarm closing in); SWORD: medium all-round. "Whiff punish" = an over-committed slow spear miss hands the deterministic swarm its free window. Express all timings as integer frames so every client agrees.

**ROW 3 — https://sekiroshadowsdietwice.wiki.fextralife.com/Perilous+Attacks** (Sekiro perilous-attack telegraph)
- **Technique:** A single red "Danger" kanji appears when an enemy begins an UNBLOCKABLE (perilous) attack — a color/symbol convention meaning "normal defense won't work here." One symbol for all three types forces reading the ANIMATION to pick the response: Thrust (shoulder drawn back, weapon pointed forward) → Mikiri counter; Sweep (left shoulder back / 180° spin, aimed at feet) → jump/stomp; Grab → dodge. Known flaw: the symbol spawns over the target and can OBSCURE the enemy's body/hands — a lesson about telegraph placement.
- **Numbers:** None quantified (symbol appears "as the enemy begins their attack"; no ms/frame value — unverified).
- **Map to bug game:** Reserve a distinct COLOR tell (red flash/outline/particle burst on the swarm) for attacks that beat the player's default defense — a surge a raised shield/parry can't stop, that must be sidestepped or netted. Keep the meaning consistent. Learn from the criticism: place the tell ON/around the swarm, never covering its wind-up (especially important top-down). Distinct animation shapes per attack type let a skilled player pick sword-vs-net-vs-dodge.

**ROW 4 — https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/enemy-attacks-and-telegraphing** (Game Developer, "Enemy Attacks and Telegraphing")
- **Technique:** Telegraphs are MULTI-SENSORY and layered: animation wind-up ("the long wind-up before a sword attack") + sound + voice-over + VFX + sometimes force-feedback. Charge attacks use accumulating VFX + rising audio + a bright release flash ("a ball of particles accumulates... 'vacuum-cleaner-in-reverse' sound... bright flash"). Even vocal/narrative cues work (Psychonauts bosses announce combos). Core principle: fairness — give the player time to respond.
- **Numbers:** NONE — no ms/frame values; verified in-fetch that it does NOT cover color conventions, small-silhouette readability, or reaction-time thresholds. Unverified for those.
- **Map to bug game:** Layer the swarm's tells for a busy cozy top-down scene — wind-up motion (swarm contracts/rears) + audio (rising buzz/hum; the "reverse-vacuum" charge idea fits a bug swarm) + small particle-gather VFX; escalate all three plus a bright flash for a big/charged attack. Redundant cues keep the tell legible when the swarm is small on-screen or partly off-view.

### Cross-source numbers that agree
- Human reaction baseline ~250ms. GDKeys pins 0.25s = ~15f @60fps / 8 @30fps; the telegraphing search corroborated ~250ms as the standard, with real telegraphs often stretched near a full second for fairness.
- 60fps ⇒ ~16.7ms/frame → any reactable swarm wind-up should be ≥~15 frames; punishable attacks noticeably longer.
- Fast attack startup ~4-5 frames; a 4-frame jab is the canonical quick punish.

### Synthesis for the bug game
1. Authored phase timing on BOTH sides — every swarm attack AND every player weapon = startup(wind-up)/active(hit)/recovery, in integer frames (deterministic-safe).
2. Wind-up is the readability contract: ≥~15f (~250ms) minimum for anything the player is expected to react to; unique, distinguishable animation per attack so weapon choice (sword/spear/net) is a read, not a guess.
3. Recovery is the counterplay dial: long on heavy/committed swarm attacks (the player's free-hit window), short on pokes (safe). Player whiffs — especially a slow spear — expose the player's own recovery = the swarm's window. This two-way frame-advantage math is what creates timing-based skill.
4. Reserve a consistent RED tell for "default defense won't work — sidestep or net it," Sekiro-style, but placed ON the swarm, never covering its wind-up (top-down makes placement critical).
5. Layer telegraphs multi-sensorially (motion + rising buzz + particle gather + release flash) so they stay legible in a cluttered, cozy scene.
