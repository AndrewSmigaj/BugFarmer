### SOURCE TABLE

**1) https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/designing-enemies-with-distinct-functions** — Harvey Smith, Game Developer
- **Technique:** Enemies need distinct *functions*, not distinct *stats*. "Reverse-engineer": decide the gameplay role first, then design the creature to fit it. Scaling speed/damage/HP alone forces no player decision.
- **Examples/numbers:** *Defender* — **Lander** (slow, descends to capture humanoids -> becomes a Mutant = resource-denial/escalation), **Mutant** (fast, erratic, aggressive pursuit = evasion), **Bomber** (slow, lays a persistent explosive trail = spatial hazard). Explicit anti-pattern: "Creature One: speed 50, 37 dmg, 100 HP" vs "Creature Two: speed 75, 12 dmg, 30 HP" — both take the identical response, so it's fake variety. Crocodile example: breath-draining underwater bite + tranquilizer-immune hide + fast-straight/slow-turn on land -> each trait removes a standard solution.
- **Bug-swarm mapping:** Each species must remove/demand a different player TOOL, not just have more HP. Author the ROLE first, then the sprite/behavior. A shelled beetle immune to the net (needs sword), a wasp only the spear's reach beats safely, a burrower the sword can't hit underground.

**2) https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/combat/enemy** — The Level Design Book (cites Mike Stout)
- **Technique:** Enemies are a VOCABULARY the player learns; a balanced roster mixes archetypes, and combinations force target-prioritization. Ranged ("Far") enemies are what make terrain/positioning matter.
- **Examples/numbers:** Six archetypes — **Grunt** (melee, easy), **Squad** (mid/long range, coordinate in groups), **Leader** (survivable, buffs allies), **Tank** (durable, slow, big hitbox), **Swarm** (fast, low-HP, many), **Sniper** (slow, weak, long-range, needs support). Mike Stout: "melee enemies by themselves are usually pretty boring"; with only Near enemies "your environment doesn't matter at all... adding Far Enemies... your level design is going to start to matter." Silhouette-readable at 10+ m; Skyrim giant's size/sluggishness says "out-maneuver and wear down." Recommends a matrix tracking speed/health/range/DPS. Each type teaches ONE idea (grunt=timing, ranged=positioning, tank=resource mgmt, swarm=area awareness); combined -> emergent difficulty via prioritization, not stat scaling.
- **Bug-swarm mapping (core of the ask):** Map species to the 6 roles — Grunt=common crawler; Sniper/Squad=spitter that pelts from range (forces closing distance or using terrain); Leader=broodmother that buffs/spawns nearby bugs (**the kill-first priority target**); Tank=armored beetle (kite/avoid); Swarm=gnat cloud (net-clearable fodder). A swarm of only crawlers is a spongey blob; add one spitter + one broodmother and the player MUST prioritize.

**3) https://www.jasondeheras.com/gamedesign/2023/12/22/enemy-design-layers-in-hollow-knight** — Jason de Heras
- **Technique:** Six layers that make each enemy READABLE and each fight distinct — Foreshadowing, Spawn type, Projectiles, Timing/Rhythm, Death states, On-hit responses. Every enemy needs a clear TELL (visual OR audio).
- **Examples/numbers:** **Mosscreep** = long visible wind-up (reaction-friendly); **Fool Eater** = weak visual tell but a distinct SHAKING SOUND (audio tell); **Mosskin** charges with ZERO startup -> play on knowledge, not reaction. Projectiles differ: **Aspid Hunter** fires AT the player, **Obble** fires a fixed X-pattern (rewards standing still). Death states: **Volatile Mosskin** explodes (delayed) on death = risk/reward on looting; **Ooma** fires fast homing shots on death -> reposition. Vengefly's dash foreshadows the Hornet boss.
- **Bug-swarm mapping:** One unmistakable tell per species, paired with its counter — a rear-back wiggle before a lunge, a color/sound flash before a spit. Death states as flavor+risk (a bombardier bug that pops a gas cloud on death — net/ranged it, don't melee). Vary attack RHYTHM across species so one dodge-timing can't clear a mixed swarm.

**4) https://deliberategamedesign.com/enemy-categorization/** — Deliberate Game Design
- **Technique:** Categorize by ROLE-relative-to-player: **Smashers** (trivial, grouped, make player feel powerful), **Emphasizers** (reward a mechanic without requiring it — should be MOST of the roster), **Enforcers** (demand a specific mechanic — use sparingly), **Challengers** (boss-tier, need multiple mechanics). Build a role×tier grid; find clusters (redundant) and gaps.
- **Examples/numbers:** Goombas = Smashers; archers/armored soldiers = Emphasizers; fire-vulnerable enemy = Enforcer; boss = Challenger. Tiers are RELATIVE to current player power. Recommended action/adventure mix: Emphasizers > Challengers > Smashers > Enforcers. A good enemy's "gimmick" fits in ONE sentence. (Does NOT cover target-prioritization or bullet-sponge directly.)
- **Bug-swarm mapping:** Audit the bestiary on a role×tier grid so you don't ship five redundant crawlers. Most species = Emphasizers (reward the right weapon, any works); a FEW Enforcers placed sparingly (flying bug ONLY the net catches; shelled bug ONLY the spear pierces) to teach the tool; gnats = Smasher power-fantasy sweeps. Every species passes the one-sentence-gimmick test.

**5) https://medium.com/@Watfen64/how-to-design-formidable-and-unforgettable-video-game-enemies-5ca8ed21fd8e** — Michel Sabbagh, Medium
- **Technique:** Two components — **Demeanor** (appearance + out-of-combat behavior telegraph threat before the fight) and **Tactics** (distinctive attacks + adaptability).
- **Examples/numbers:** Left 4 Dead Special Infected as the model roster — **Boomer** (bile that attracts a horde = support/debuffer), **Tank** (heavy punches = bruiser), **Jockey** (clings to and steers survivors = controller/disabler). BioShock Big Daddy escorts a Little Sister out-of-combat (identity before the fight). Shadow of Mordor's Nemesis system = enemies remember the player and adapt. (Does NOT cover numbers, bullet-sponge, or prioritization.)
- **Bug-swarm mapping:** L4D is the cleanest template for distinct-role swarm members: a "Boomer" bug (bursts a pheromone that summons/enrages more bugs -> kill at range/first), a "Jockey" bug that latches and slows/steers the player (peel it off), a bruiser beetle. Out-of-combat idle demeanor reads the threat (spitter rearing, swarm massing) so the cozy player still gets a fair warning.

### Synthesis — making a bug swarm interesting, not spongy
- **Root rule (Harvey Smith):** differentiate species by FUNCTION/counter, never by HP. A spongey swarm is one where every bug wants the same response.
- **Give the roster ROLES** (Level Design Book's 6 + L4D's controller/support): Grunt (crawler), Ranged/Sniper (spitter — makes terrain matter), Buffer/Leader (broodmother — kill-first target), Tank (armored beetle — kite), Swarm (gnats — net fodder), plus 1-2 controllers/debuffers (latcher, pheromone-popper). Mixing these is what creates prioritization.
- **One readable tell per species** (Hollow Knight): wind-up wiggle, color/sound flash, telegraphed charge — and vary attack RHYTHM so one dodge-timing can't clear a mixed swarm.
- **Map each species to a player TOOL:** net = airborne/fragile swarmers; spear = reach vs spitters & shelled bugs; sword = burst vs tanks/broodmothers. A few "Enforcer" species that only one tool beats teach the kit; most are "Emphasizers."
- **Audit on a role×tier grid** (Deliberate GD) so no two species are redundant; every species passes the one-sentence-gimmick test.
