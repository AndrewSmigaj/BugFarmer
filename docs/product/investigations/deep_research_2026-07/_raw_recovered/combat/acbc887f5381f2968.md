## CLUSTER 2 RESEARCH: Tool-specific use motions in acclaimed 2D games

### Search angles run (≥4, named)
- **By-tool:** "shovel dig scoop vs axe chop pickaxe swing"; "watering can tilt spout pour frames".
- **By-game:** "Stardew tool animation frames sprite sheet"; "Terraria swing/stab shortsword vs broadsword"; "Core Keeper/Dinkum top-down mining swing"; "Graveyard Keeper/Portia shovel/pickaxe".
- **By-source-type:** wiki animation/modding pages (Stardew `Modding:Farmer_sprite`, Terraria `Use_Style_IDs`), a pixel-art dev blog (SLYNYRD), animation-principle guides (MoCap Online), an action-game mechanics essay (canmom.art), and **open-source code** (a Godot Stardew-like).
- **By-problem:** "make digging feel good / tool-use game feel"; "wind-up anticipation follow-through arc procedural top-down".

### Key finding that reframes the whole task
Every acclaimed exemplar I could read — Stardew, Terraria, and the open-source Godot farm — **bakes the tool motion into per-direction art frames** (4- or 8-directional sprite sheets), OR quantizes aim to 4 directions and plays a canned clip. The "scoop vs chop" distinction lives in the *pixels*, not in a transform. **No top-down farming game I found synthesizes distinct tool motions from transforms of one icon** — and notably **none shows a genuine shovel "scoop"**; Dinkum/Core Keeper/Graveyard Keeper reuse a generic swing/poke for the shovel, and Terraria's Gravedigger's Shovel just swings like a sword. So the owner's ask is both a real gap in the genre and something we largely have to *design from real motion + animation principles*, not copy.

Because we move only the tool icon (no body/arm/hand recue, no per-frame art), the tool's transform must carry 100% of readability. Our only design levers are: **arc plane (along-aim vs across-aim), arc direction (down / sideways / up-lift), wind-up duration, contact-beat type (impact-HOLD vs DRAG-back vs SWEEP-through vs POUR-hold), forward lunge, and scale-for-depth.** The distinction between tools must come from *combining these differently*.

### PER-TOOL MOTION SPEC (for our rig: pivot at player center, aim = mouse vector `A`, `P` = 90° perpendicular; grip = bottom-left, head = top-right of icon)

All phases are transform-only (rotate + translate + scale). "Contact beat" = where SFX/particles/hit register.

**1. SHOVEL — dig/scoop (in-then-lever-up, the owner's exemplar)**
- Phase 1 Set (120ms): lift + tilt blade forward-down, small back-cock. Scale ~1.0.
- Phase 2 **Thrust-in (80ms):** translate head **forward along `A` + into the ground**, blade-first; **scale DOWN ~0.9** (reads as going into/away). **← contact beat (bite).**
- Phase 3 **Scoop-lift (140ms):** **rotate head UP-and-back toward player about the grip** (the lever/pry), translate slightly back along `-A`, **scale UP ~1.1** (reads as lifting toward viewer). This up-arc is what separates it from a chop.
- Phase 4 Toss/settle (120ms): ease to idle.
- Direction: **along-aim thrust, then a vertical-ish up-rotation** — never a sideways sweep. Small forward lunge on Phase 2.

**2. AXE / PICKAXE — chop/mine (overhead down-strike + HOLD)**
- Phase 1 **Wind-up (180–250ms, heavy):** rotate head up/**overhead behind** (away from `A`), translate back. Slow.
- Phase 2 **Strike (60–90ms):** fast rotate + translate head **DOWN along `A`** onto target; ease so peak velocity ~60–70% of the arc. **← contact beat at arc bottom.**
- Phase 3 **Impact HOLD (~200ms):** hold the struck pose (Stardew holds 220ms); hook screenshake/spark here.
- Phase 4 Recoil/recover (75–120ms): small rebound up, ease to idle.
- Direction: **downward rotational arc in the aim plane.** Small lunge. Total ~600–800ms (heavy).

**3. HOE — till (down-strike + DRAG-back)**
- Phase 1 Raise (120ms): rotate head up/back ~45° (shallower than axe).
- Phase 2 **Chop-down (70ms):** rotate + translate head down onto soil along `A`. **← contact beat.**
- Phase 3 **Drag-pull (120ms):** translate the whole sprite **back toward player along `-A`** (the turn-soil drag) with slight rotation — this DRAG is the hoe's signature vs the axe's static hold.
- Phase 4 Recover (100ms).
- Direction: down-then-pull-back along aim.

**4. SCYTHE / NET — sweep (wide across-aim arc — closest to what we have)**
- Phase 1 **Wind-up (80ms):** rotate head to one side (`-P`).
- Phase 2 **Sweep (fast):** rotate the sprite through a **wide arc across the front, `-P` → through `A` → `+P`** (~180°), slight forward translate at mid-sweep; peak velocity mid-arc. Contact spread across the arc (AoE).
- Phase 3 **Follow-through (past `+P`) → ease back.**
- Direction: **sideways arc centered on aim, in the ground plane.** Keep our current swing but WIDEN it, add the opposite-side wind-up and a follow-through so it reads as a sweep, not a poke. No lunge. This is what our rig does best.

**5. WATERING CAN — pour (tilt + long HOLD, NOT a swing)**
- Phase 1 Present (120ms): translate can forward along `A`, small lift.
- Phase 2 **Tilt-to-pour (100ms):** rotate so the **spout (head) tips DOWN-forward** toward the ground (~−45° to −60°).
- Phase 3 **Sustained-pour HOLD (~400–500ms):** hold the tilted pose still; **water particle FX emit here** — this long still hold is the entire readable signature and is what makes watering feel unlike every strike tool.
- Phase 4 Tip-back to idle (100ms).
- Direction: static forward tilt; **no arc, no lunge, no impact** — the "beat" is the pour hold.

**6. SWORD — slash (fast across-aim arc + lunge)**
- Phase 1 **Wind-up (80–120ms, light):** cock head to `-P` + slightly back.
- Phase 2 **Slash (fast):** wide arc `-P` → through `A` → `+P` **with a forward LUNGE (translate whole rig along `A`)**; peak velocity ~60–70% of arc; contact peaks at `A`.
- Phase 3 **Follow-through past `+P` → recover.** Alternate the lead side each swing for a combo feel.
- Direction: horizontal/diagonal arc centered on aim **+ lunge** (this lunge is what distinguishes sword from the stationary scythe sweep). Total ~400ms.

### Cross-tool signature (so six tools read as six motions with only transforms)
- **Down-strike family (axe/pick/hoe):** overhead/back wind-up → fast DOWN arc along aim → *axe/pick = impact HOLD*, *hoe = DRAG back*.
- **Sweep family (scythe/sword/net):** opposite-side wind-up → wide arc ACROSS aim → follow-through; *sword adds a forward LUNGE*, scythe/net do not.
- **Shovel:** the odd one — THRUST along aim → rotational LIFT up-and-back, with scale-down-then-up for fake depth.
- **Watering:** the other odd one — TILT then a long still POUR HOLD, no strike at all.
Distinguishers are: arc plane (down/along vs across), the terminal beat (HOLD vs DRAG vs SWEEP-through vs POUR-hold), and presence of a lunge.

### LIMITS — what can't be done with rotation + translation + scale of one flat sprite (flag these)
1. **True out-of-plane 3D motion** — a real blade cupping/scooping, or an axe head rotating toward/away from camera — is impossible; we only *fake depth via scale* (smaller = into ground/away, larger = lifted toward viewer). The shovel scoop and any "into the screen" thrust are approximations.
2. **No smear frames / squash-stretch / blade bend** — the pro sources (SLYNYRD, MoCap Online) lean on smears and deform to sell speed; with no per-frame art we can only fake a smear with an **afterimage/motion-blur FX layer** or a **stretch-scale along the motion axis**, not sprite deformation.
3. **No body/arm/hand recue** — Stardew, Terraria and the Godot game all reposition the hand/grip or swap hands and lean the torso to sell the motion. We move only the icon, so the tool's transform must carry *all* readability — this raises the required arc width and wind-up clarity (subtle motions will read as nothing).
4. **Asymmetric tools may need a horizontal MIRROR (flip), which is beyond pure rotation** — the watering can's spout and any handled tool can rotate to a backwards/upside-down read for left-facing aim; a sign-flip on scale-x (mirror) fixes it but is an extra allowance to build in.
5. **Sustained states need a HOLD in the tween, not a one-shot arc** — watering's pour (and optionally a held dig) require the animator to *pause* at a pose for a fixed duration; make sure the tween system supports a dwell, not just start→end interpolation.
